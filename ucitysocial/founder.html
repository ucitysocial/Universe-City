    2015 to 2026

    Timeline

    2015 to 2020
    I work in multifamily interior design. I see how much money goes into building housing. I cannot work out why living in it keeps costing more.
    2016
    My mom dies. I am 20.
    2018
    I start a podcast named Blank Univercity. I design its first logo myself. Then I pay an artist to redraw it.
    2021
    I move from Dallas to Denver.
    2023
    I present a proposal to the Denver Housing Authority. It has twelve areas, each named after a constellation.
    2022 to 2025
    I work in community management, at a nonprofit for homeless youth, and at a community radio station. I attend culinary school. Each job changes how I define the folders.
    January 2026
    I expand the first model of twelve folders into four departments with twelve folders each.
    Now
    I am 30. I am the first resident of Universe City. I run all forty eight folders on my own life first, in public. I publish the numbers as I go.

    Chapter 01 · 2016

    What was lost

    My mom just knew me.

    She was always someone I could call. She checked in on me. She reminded me that I was loved, but she also reminded me who I was. She knew my strengths and my weaknesses. She was the best hugger in the world, and she had the best laugh.

    She had her own struggles with alcohol and mental health, but I still knew what it felt like to have a mother who knew me that deeply.

    When she died, it left this huge hole in my chest.

    It also created a contrast that is still hard for me to explain. I knew I had to adapt quickly, but there was nothing quick about it. It has taken me almost ten years, and I think some parts of it may take another ten.

    Over time, I started thinking about all the things people had complimented me on. I was well mannered. I was organized. I thought in systems.

    A lot of that did not come from me sitting down and deciding to become those things. I learned it passively from her.

    That became part of the reason for Universe City.

    I want people to have help building that kind of structure into their own lives, even if they did not grow up with a mom like mine.

    Chapter 02

    What grief did to my life

    After my mom died, I started pushing people away.

    I ended a long term relationship. I kept friends at arm’s length. I developed an unhealthy relationship with work.

    And to be frank, I was angry at God.

    I remember thinking, I guess anything goes now.

    The first few years after losing her were extremely distracting. I worked a lot. I made bad decisions. I stayed busy.

    But I was also trying to make sense of it.

    I do not think I was searching for anything.

    I think I was trying to heal using the same tools I had always used to soothe myself.

    Creating. Planning. Performing. Building ideas. Working.

    Those things were already part of me long before grief.

    As a kid I had a dog walking business. If we went to the pool, I wanted to sell snacks and Slurpees. If my siblings and I wanted something special, my mom would make us create PowerPoint presentations. We had to think it through, make a plan, and present it.

    I loved that.

    When I was around 10, I would role play owning a homeless shelter instead of playing with Barbies. In fourth grade, I wanted to open a daycare in our garage. A year later, I was calling wood shops from the Yellow Pages asking if they had spare wood because I was convinced I could build my own car.

    I was always asking for a camera or a computer for Christmas or my birthday. We were filming skits when we were seven. I kept doing that through high school, and I still do it now.

    Singing was the same way. I wanted to be a huge performer, but even then a big part of the dream was making enough money to retire my mom. I did not even really understand retirement yet.

    At career day, I would flip to the back of the books and look for the highest salaries. In sixth grade I decided I was going to be an anesthesiologist because it made the most money in the book.

    I loved animals too. I was always bringing something home or calling a bird sanctuary if I found an injured bird.

    I had a great childhood.

    Creating, planning, caring, building, performing, researching, and trying to own something were already part of me.

    After my mom died, I naturally reached for those same things.

    Eventually I realized they were not enough.

    Chapter 03 · 2018

    Blank Univercity

    In 2018, I started the Blank Univercity podcast.

    Creativity had always been one of the ways I understood myself, so I used the podcast to say out loud what I did not know how to organize anywhere else.

    I talked about love, sex, death, grief, feeling lost, feeling confused.

    It helped.

    But it was still an outlet.

    I could express what I was feeling creatively, and I was still struggling to build a life around what I was learning about myself.

    Around that same time, I became obsessed with community development corporations and housing.

    I was researching ways people could own homes and lower their cost of living. I kept coming back to modular housing and manufactured homes because I thought the stigma around them was getting in the way of a real ownership option.

    I talked about it so much that an operations executive I worked with gave me 4 percent equity in a land acquisition deal based on the research I had been doing.

    That should probably tell you how much I was talking about housing.

    I had a lot of ideas.

    Housing. Songwriting. Podcasts. Creative projects. Work. Usually more than one job at a time.

    I was interested in everything.

    I was also exhausted.

    Chapter 04

    Structure, sports, and why I kept rebuilding

    I did not think of my life as a system.

    I just knew that when things felt off, I needed structure.

    I usually started with a calendar.

    Then sleep. Wake up on time. Go to the gym. Eat better. Read. Get back into some kind of routine.

    A lot of that came from sports.

    I grew up in a pretty athletic family, and sports taught me that if you want to win, you have to train like a winner.

    You do not just show up on game day and hope it works out.

    At first, my systems were printouts I hung on the wall.

    I would have one in the kitchen for dishes and groceries. Another one for reading, practicing singing, cleaning my bathroom, or whatever I was trying to stay consistent with.

    I loved making them.

    The problem was keeping them up.

    I think that almost made it easier to let chaos build, because I knew I could clean it up.

    Especially in the earlier years, I would track it. Two weeks would go by and I was still doing what I said I was going to do.

    One time I remember realizing, oh my God, I am on week eight and I have not fallen off.

    I was not binging again. I was not letting my home get super messy again.

    So I knew I could do it.

    And then eventually it would happen again.

    Chapter 05 · 2023 to 2026

    From twelve buckets to the current structure

    Parts of me felt like a lot of adults were already gone.

    They were buried in work, distracted, exhausted, did not care, or I felt like the matrix had already swallowed them whole.

    So naturally I started thinking about the kids.

    How do we make this something our young ones can learn early so they do not need it as supplemental education once they get to the point of having to manage it all?

    That thinking showed up in earlier versions of Universe City.

    At first, I tried to fit everything into twelve little buckets.

    The twelve areas were tied to the stars and the zodiac, and the work was much more focused on family management, education, community development, and life skills.

    I kept stretching those twelve areas to cover more and more of life.

    Activism turned into several different kinds of work. Family started carrying responsibilities that clearly belonged in separate places.

    The more I built, the more obvious it became that twelve was not enough.

    Read the August 2023 edition of Universe City News

    By 2026, I was burnt out from community radio and from constantly getting distracted by other organizations’ missions while putting Universe City off to the side.

    I turned 30 in December 2025, and something in me was like, hey, stop neglecting this.

    I was financially comfortable. I lived by myself in a nice apartment in North Boulder. I was working ungodly hours. I had jobs that sounded cool. I was making decent money.

    I still felt unfulfilled.

    I kept thinking the next thing would fill the hole and it did not.

    I had spent years repeating the same habit of work horsing and staying on go. At one point I had two jobs and was in culinary school at the same time.

    So at the beginning of 2026, I decided to take a real risk.

    I quit my job with no backup.

    I wanted to believe the universe had my back. I also wanted to put myself in the exact position this operating system was supposed to help someone manage.

    No job. Rent due. Moving out. No clear idea what came next.

    And I locked in.

    I downsized my wardrobe. I bought refillable items for what I used every day. I limited my resources down to what I actually needed. I packed my apartment into storage.

    Somewhere in the middle of all that, the four departments started to appear.

    Housing was the first one I knew had to stand on its own.

    It took forever to figure out what belonged inside it. At one point the folders were literally rooms. Living room. Kitchen. Bedroom.

    That did not feel right.

    Career came next.

    After quitting my job, I started thinking about everything that goes into getting a job, keeping a job, and actually excelling at work.

    I also had a lot to pull from. I had worked more than 30 jobs by the time I was 30. I felt like I had done every job under the sun.

    Life Management was already there.

    Standards was the first folder in Life Management, and it still is.

    Standards was what made me admit something I had been avoiding.

    I do not actually want to work here.

    I do not want to keep doing this.

    Then came Personal Assessment.

    Once my own life got rocked, I did what I always do.

    I started asking: what does my schedule look like? What time am I going to sleep? What time am I waking up? Where are my documents? What am I eating? What am I doing to stay healthy?

    That later became Agency Assessment because it became the place agents and residents begin during enrollment. It is the assessment of where a resident stands in those twelve areas before getting into the others.

    The exact folders will keep developing.

    The structure did not arrive fully formed.

    It came from trying to make real life fit into the model, realizing where it did not fit, and changing the model.
