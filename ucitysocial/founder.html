Brittnay Folkes · Founder

Why I built Universe City.

I did not start with a productivity app. I started with a question: what does it take to keep a life from falling through the cracks when one person is expected to carry all of it?

the beginning

Losing my mom changed what adulthood meant.

I lost my guiding light at 20, right before turning 21.

I did not just lose someone I loved. I lost the person I could call when I did not know what to do, the person who knew my history without needing an explanation, and the person who made the world feel less impossible to navigate.

After that, adulthood felt enormous. Time, money, work, health, housing, decisions, standards, paperwork, relationships, and all of the small things that keep life from unraveling were suddenly mine to understand and manage.

01 · PREPAREDNESS

I stopped asking why adulthood felt hard and started asking what people need to be prepared for it.

Working with young people changed the question for me. I started thinking about what someone should understand before they are ever expected to manage adulthood alone.

What should they know about time? Money? Health? Home? Work? Standards? Responsibilities? What information should already be organized? What decisions should not have to be made from scratch every single time?

The problem was bigger than knowing more. People needed structure around what they already had to manage.

02 · FROM TOPICS TO SYSTEMS

A list of life skills was not enough.

I spent years breaking everyday life apart and looking at what actually keeps it functioning. A schedule is not useful because someone once taught you time management. It is useful because it stays current. A budget is not useful because you know what one is. It is useful because the numbers reflect the life you are living now.

That changed Universe City from an idea about education into an idea about maintained systems. The goal became less about telling people what they should know and more about helping them keep the important parts of their life organized, current, and usable.

03 · WHY AN AGENT

I did not want to build another tool people had to manage by themselves.

Most life management tools still leave the management work with the person using them. You have to remember to open the app, update the list, rebuild the plan, notice what changed, and figure out what needs your attention next.

Universe City is an agency because I wanted responsibility to live somewhere. Your systems hold the working details. Your agent helps maintain them, follows up on unfinished work, prepares the next decision, and keeps the plan connected when one part of life changes another.

The resident should not have to manage the system that is supposed to help manage their life.

what that became

A life management agency for everyday people.

Professional athletes and entertainers often have people around them whose job is to keep track of the moving parts. Most people are expected to do all of that invisible coordination alone.

Universe City is built around a different assumption: everyday life is complex enough to deserve management support too.

01You make the decisions that belong to you.
02Your agent carries the administrative work around those decisions.
03Open work stays active until it is handled, changed, or intentionally dismissed.
04Your systems stay current so you do not start over every time life changes.

the name

Universe big. City small.

I kept thinking about problems that are so large they become abstract: instability, hunger, housing, education, work, access, and the basic difficulty of keeping a life together.

The name became a way of shrinking the question until there was something a person or a city could actually work on.

What does it take to create a better world? It takes U in your CITY.

Universe City is still answering the same question.

How do we make everyday life easier to understand, easier to manage, and harder to lose track of? The answer I am building is one resident, one system, one commitment, and one city at a time.
