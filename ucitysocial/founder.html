    Chapter 01 · 2016

    I was 20.

    My mom died in 2016. I was 20, heading into 21.

    She was the person I could call. She was the person I could move back in with. She had known me since the day I was born.

    She taught me how to handle parts of everyday life before I knew how to manage them myself.

    After she died, I understood how much administrative work she had been doing with me. She helped me keep information in order. She helped me make decisions. She helped me handle responsibilities I had not learned to manage yet.

    I was now the person responsible for understanding myself as fully as she did.

    Chapter 02

    Adulthood felt enormous.

    I was now responsible for every part of my own life.

    I did not have clear standards for myself. I made decisions without a plan. No person could tell me what deserved my attention first.

    Parents often do part of this work for their children. Some people lose that support. Some people never have it.

    I knew I was not the only person who felt that way.

    Chapter 03 · 2018

    Blank Univercity

    In 2018 I started a podcast. I named it Blank Univercity.

    I was creating from grief. I was processing in public what had happened to me.

    On the podcast I joked that grief had sent me to Street University. The joke was about becoming emotionally hardened. I felt tougher and more guarded than I had been before my mom died.

    I designed the first logo myself. Then I paid an artist to redraw it.

    Blank Univercity was tied to a social media identity I no longer use. One question from the podcast stayed with me.

    What are people expected to know about adulthood, even though nobody formally teaches it?

    The name Universe City came from Blank Univercity.

    Chapter 04

    Working with kids changed the question.

    I worked with kids at two organizations. One was a nonprofit for homeless youth.

    I started asking what a child should understand before adulthood begins.

    I started thinking in terms of preparedness.

    What do these kids need to understand to be prepared for adulthood?

    Chapter 05

    What makes a life easier to manage?

    For years I asked versions of that one question.

    What does a person need to write down? What needs a schedule? What needs a rule? What needs a plan?

    My mom was an engineer. She thought in systems. I recognize the same instinct in myself.

    I inherited her instinct to look for structure.

    Chapter 06

    The constellations

    My mom loved the moon and the constellations.

    As Blank Univercity became Universe City, I used the constellations as structure. Each department has 12 folders. Each folder position matches one of the 12 zodiac constellations. Aries is Folder 01.

    The constellations exist whether or not a person believes in astrology.

    I built this structure myself. I used the constellations to build it.

    Chapter 07

    Where I come from

    Both of my parents were Libras. They were born on the same day.

    Law and order were present in my upbringing. So were dysfunction, inconsistency, and the outlaw side of life.

    I can see my upbringing in Universe City. It has folders named Standards, Mediation, Boundary, and Law.

    I know what life is like when rules and records are inconsistent or missing.

    Chapter 08 · The name

    Universe big. City small.

    I kept thinking about problems that are too large for one person to solve. Hunger is one. Homelessness is one.

    I asked myself a question. What is it going to take to create a better world?

    It is going to take U in your CITY.

    The universe is big. A city is small enough for one person to act in.

    Chapter 09 · 2026

    I am 30 now.

    I have spent most of my twenties building a way to make everyday life easier to manage.

    I started Universe City in 2018. I am still its first resident.

    Universe City provides life management. It does not provide therapy. It does not provide case management.

    It provides the structure of the help a parent gives. It does not replace the relationship.

    In the long term, I want practical life management education to be available in schools and communities.
