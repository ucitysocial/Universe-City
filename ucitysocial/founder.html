Why I built Universe City

Brittnay Folkes · Denver

01
Chapter 01 · 2016

Losing my guiding light

I was 20, heading into 21, when my mom died suddenly.

What shook me first was not losing somebody who handled my appointments or organized my life. I lost my guiding light. My support system. The person I could call, fall back on, move back in with, love on and hug.

I lost the one person who had known me from the beginning of time.

I realized I would now be the person responsible for understanding myself as fully as she had.
02
Chapter 02 · Adulthood

Everything felt like so much.

Finances. Expenses. Managing my time. Managing my goals and priorities. Knowing what to focus on.

I did not have clear standards for myself. I could act on a whim because I did not have a plan strong enough to return to.

There were no clear signs telling me which direction to go. No single plan for what adulthood was supposed to look like.

None of these things were extraordinary on their own. Together, they felt enormous.

And I knew I could not be the only person who felt that way.

03
Chapter 03 · Blank University

Street University

Blank University · podcast era

At the time, I was creating from grief and trying to process what had happened to me. On my podcast, I used to joke that I had attended “Street University.”

It was my way of saying grief had hardened me. I had gone through one of life’s hardest lessons and came out feeling tougher, more guarded and, jokingly, a little more like a thug than the person I had been before it.

Blank University began as part of an old social media identity. The idea underneath it stayed with me: adulthood asks people to know things nobody formally teaches.

04
Chapter 04 · Preparedness

The question changed when I started thinking about kids.

I wanted to work with kids. That made me think about adulthood from the opposite direction.

Instead of asking why I was struggling with certain things, I started asking what a kid should understand before adulthood so those things would not feel impossible later.

What do these kids need to have a firm grip on to be prepared for adulthood?

Time. Money. Health. Home. Work. Standards. Relationships. Responsibilities.

I started thinking in terms of preparedness.

05
Chapter 05 · Systems

I started studying the structure underneath everyday life.

My mom was an engineer. She had a systems brain. I do not mean that she was quietly running an administrative system for my life. She was my mom.

But I recognize that systems instinct in myself.

For years, I kept asking the same questions in different forms: What makes a life easier to manage? What needs a schedule? What needs a rule? What needs to be written down? What needs to be maintained instead of repeatedly rebuilt?

I have spent the better part of ten years trying to understand how the pieces of everyday life fit together well enough to make them easier to manage.
06
Chapter 06 · The stars

The constellations became part of the architecture.

My mom loved the moon and the constellations.

As Blank University became Universe City, I began using the constellations as structure. Each department has 12 folders. Each folder position reflects one of the 12 zodiac constellations, beginning with Aries at Folder 01.

010203040506 070809101112

Whether somebody relates to zodiac or astrology readings is beside the point. The constellations exist. Their existence became my devotion to the natural powers that be — my way of saying: I know I put this together, but I turned to the stars to make it so.

07
Chapter 07 · Upbringing

Law, order, dysfunction and the outlaw.

Both of my parents were Libras. They were born on the same day.

I find that telling. I grew up around law and order, but outlaw energy and dysfunction visited our home too.

That contrast shaped me.

I can see it inside Universe City now: rules, standards, mediation, boundaries, records, law and structure — built by somebody who also knows what life feels like when structure is inconsistent, absent or simply not enough.

Brittnay Folkes · founder

08
Chapter 08 · The name

Universe big. City small.

Universe City became the name when the idea got bigger.

I wanted the work to be able to hold more than one person’s schedule or budget. I kept thinking about the largest problems people talk about: hunger, homelessness, instability, education, work.

UNIVERSE big
CITY small

I would say, “What’s it going to take to create a better world?”

“It’s going to take U in your CITY.”

The name became a way of taking something enormous and zooming in until it became personal enough to act on.

09
Chapter 09 · Now

I am 30 now.

I have spent most of my twenties trying to build something that makes life easier to understand and easier to manage.

Universe City began with grief. It grew through questions about preparedness. It took shape through years of studying the ordinary responsibilities people are expected to carry.

And it became a city because I stopped believing the only useful way to think about big problems was at their biggest scale.

Sometimes the better question is smaller: What does one person need? What can we organize? What can we understand? What can we make easier from here?
