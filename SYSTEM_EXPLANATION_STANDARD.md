# Universe City System Explanation Standard

This document defines how Universe City explains its systems on the public site, inside product education, in sales language, in training, and in future system documentation.

The goal is not to make every system sound impressive. The goal is to make each system understandable.

## Core principle

Every explanation should blend what the system is with how a resident actually uses it.

A definition alone is not enough.

A feature list alone is not enough.

An agent job description alone is not enough.

A fictional scenario alone is not enough.

The reader should come away understanding the practical object they are using, how it fits into their life, how their Universe City agent participates, and how the system stays useful over time.

## The public explanation has two layers

### In Universe City

This explains what the system becomes inside Universe City.

It should answer:

1. What is this system for?
2. What part of life does it organize, maintain, plan, compare, or clarify?
3. What does the resident use it to accomplish?
4. What makes the Universe City version different from simply keeping notes or using a generic app?

This section should be concise. It is the functional explanation.

### What that looks like

This paints the picture of using the system.

It should answer some combination of:

1. What brings the resident into the system?
2. What does the resident actually do with it?
3. How does the Universe City agent participate?
4. What information gets discussed, confirmed, organized, compared, or changed?
5. What happens after that interaction?
6. How does repeated use make the system more current, realistic, useful, or aligned with the resident's goals?

This is not a place for a random fictional scenario. It should reveal the normal rhythm of the system.

## The interaction model

The systems should not all use the same visible formula.

Internally, however, each explanation should account for four ideas:

1. Use. What does the resident use this system for?
2. Interaction. What does working with the system actually involve?
3. Agent contribution. What does the Universe City agent help notice, organize, clarify, compare, maintain, or plan?
4. Development. How does the system become more useful as the resident keeps using it and their life changes?

These ideas may appear in a different order for every folder.

Do not turn them into repeated sentence stems.

## Different systems have different rhythms

Universe City is not forty eight forms that residents are expected to maintain in the same way.

Time may be used for regular planning across days, weeks, and months.

Health may become active around appointments, symptoms, follow up, or changes in care.

Inventory may change when items are purchased, used, replaced, moved, or replenished.

Salary may become important when pay, hours, costs, responsibilities, or job options change.

Standards may become relevant when a decision, opportunity, relationship, or conflict tests a condition the resident has already decided matters.

The explanation should teach the rhythm that belongs to that system.

## The agent belongs inside the picture

The agent should not be added as a decorative phrase.

When the agent appears in public copy, the reader should understand what working together actually means.

Depending on the system, that may include planning, asking a focused question, making sense of what happened, comparing information, carrying unfinished items forward, preparing for a decision, noticing a pattern, keeping information current, or refining the next plan.

The resident is not expected to maintain the entire system alone.

At the same time, do not make every paragraph say that the resident returns and reviews something with their agent. The interaction must sound natural for the system being explained.

## Maintenance and progress

Universe City is designed to keep systems useful after they are first created.

The purpose of continued engagement is not engagement for its own sake.

Residents use a system, live their lives, and interact with it again when that system becomes relevant. Universe City maintains continuity between those moments.

Over time, systems can be tracked, maintained, and refined as the resident progresses.

Public language should make this continuity understandable without exposing internal process language or making the product sound robotic.

## Homepage versus department pages

The homepage introduces the system.

A homepage explanation should be short enough to answer what it is and why it matters.

A department page is the next layer.

It should not repeat the homepage in more words. It should add the missing how.

Department copy should show the resident's interaction with the system, the agent's role when relevant, and how the system develops through use.

## Writing rules

1. Be specific about the object. Say schedule, household inventory, pay picture, standards, maintenance log, or another concrete system when that is what it is.
2. Explain ordinary use before advanced capability.
3. Do not inflate a simple system into an intelligence product.
4. Do not make Universe City sound like a chatbot, therapy app, generic productivity app, or passive database.
5. Avoid vague openers such as "when something changes" when the actual thing can be named.
6. Avoid canned fictional scenarios such as "your work schedule changes next week" unless a specific scenario is genuinely the clearest explanation.
7. Do not use phrases such as "living plan" unless the next sentence immediately explains what makes it living.
8. Do not begin What that looks like with empty framing such as "you can see." Describe the interaction.
9. Vary the cadence and verbs across systems. Different folders should feel like different parts of a real agency.
10. Use resident facing language on public pages. Internal architecture may use file, case, record, desk, and Genesis. Public explanations should not require residents to understand those structures.
11. Do not use hyphens, en dash punctuation, or em dash punctuation in public copy. Rewrite with commas, colons, semicolons, periods, or a different sentence structure.
12. Keep the language potent. More depth does not automatically mean more words.

## Canonical Time example

### In Universe City

Time is the schedule you use to plan your days, weeks, and months. It brings together the commitments already shaping your life with the commitments you want to make as you work toward your goals. Universe City helps keep that schedule current and realistic as your life changes.

### What that looks like

You plan with Time, live the plan, then check back in with your Universe City agent to make sense of what actually happened. Maybe something took longer than expected, a commitment changed, or something never got done. That information is carried into the next plan so your schedule becomes easier to trust and more useful as you progress.

## Why the Time example works

It names the actual product: a schedule.

It explains its scale: days, weeks, and months.

It includes the life that already exists and the life the resident is trying to build.

It shows what the resident does instead of only describing what the system stores.

It gives the agent a real role.

It shows continuity between one plan and the next.

It explains progress without claiming the schedule becomes magically intelligent.

## Applying the standard to other systems

Do not copy the Time paragraph and replace the nouns.

For each folder, first answer these questions in plain language:

1. What is the actual thing the resident uses?
2. Why would they open or interact with it?
3. What do they do there?
4. When would their agent naturally become involved?
5. What would the agent help them do?
6. What information changes through that interaction?
7. What becomes easier, clearer, more current, or more intentional the next time the system is used?

Only after those questions are answered should public copy be written.

## Final test

Before publishing an explanation, ask:

Could a person who has never seen Universe City picture themselves using this?

Do they know what the system actually is?

Do they understand how they would interact with it?

Does the agent have a useful role rather than a ceremonial one?

Does the explanation show how the system stays useful over time?

Does it sound different from the other forty seven systems where it should?

If the answer to any of these is no, the explanation is not finished.
