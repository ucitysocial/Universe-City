**1.2**  The other four. **Folder 01 Time** states what the folder is. **Folder 01 Time Operations** holds the fields, the statuses, the permissions, and the contact settings. **Folder 01 Time Research** holds the evidence. **Folder 01 Time Developments** holds the dated log of changes.

**1.3**  This document does not restate the other four. Where a rule is held in one of them, this document names the clause.

**1.4**  Where this document and Folder 01 Time disagree, Folder 01 Time governs. The Language Standard governs the wording of both.

**1.5**  The questions, the options, the lines beneath them, the definitions, and what each lesson says are in the official layer of the Language Standard. They are never reworded for a client. Language Standard 4.3 and 4.13.4.

**1.6**  Guided Life is an optional way to carry out a Plan with the guide. It is no subject of assessment and it adds no question. Folder 01 Time, 5.11.

**1.7**  Version 2 is dated October 11, 2026. It replaces Version 1 of August 28, 2026. Version 1 asked how well each of twelve kinds of time was accounted for. Version 2 asks how a client plans a commitment, carries it out, reviews what happened, and follows through on the work that remains.

**1.8**  The twelve subjects of Version 1 are kept. They moved to the Schedule intake in section 18. They are no longer the twelve questions.

**1.9**  The twelve questions have not been tested with clients. Folder 01 Time Research states that related research does not validate them. Section 22 lists what a test would settle.

# 2. What the scale measures
**2.1**  The scale measures how a client plans a commitment, carries it out, reviews what happened, and follows through on the work that remains. Each one is measured under the conditions the client set. Folder 01 Time, 2.3 and 2.6.

**2.2**  Four measures are never used.

**2.2.1  The number of commitments.** A Plan with two commitments and a Plan with ten can sit at the same option. Rest counts the same as every other commitment. Folder 01 Time, 2.7.

**2.2.2  Exact punctuality.** A start or a finish is compared with the timing the client set. No punctuality rule applies to every client.

**2.2.3  Doing it with no support.** A reminder, the schedule, a list, a calendar, the agent, the guide, a person who helps the client, and work done under a standing permission count at every option.

**2.2.4  How much the client writes down.** The agent keeps the file. No option asks the client to keep a log.

**2.3**  Each scale measures one ability. Each question states its progression: the one measure that rises, and the ten levels in order, one for each option. A higher option is a higher level of that ability. It is never a second ability.

**2.4**  Option 1 states that the subject is not functioning. Option 10 states that the subject worked through the past month under the client’s conditions, and what happened to an exception. Option 10 does not require a month with no exception. Language Standard 9.6.

**2.5**  No option requires a condition that an employer, an institution, or a second person sets for the client. Each option is written so that it is true inside those conditions. A client can reach every option, and can select option 10 as a Target, inside conditions that other people set. Language Standard 9.8.2.

**2.6**  An option is true when it is true with the support the client uses. A client who looks at the schedule, asks the agent, uses Guided Life, or has work done under a standing permission meets the option. The client’s own part is to report, to decide, to give a permission, and to do the action only the client can do. Folder 01 Time, 5.5.6.

**2.7**  Agent assistance is never a lower level. No option ranks doing a task alone above doing it with the agent, with the guide, with a reminder, or with work done under a standing permission. Language Standard 9.8.1.

**2.8**  About half and most describe a share of the client’s own planned commitments. They never describe a count.

**2.9**  Start and Target describe the same level at each number. Target 4 is Start 4 stated as a condition the client chooses. Target 1 is stated as dealing with the subject when it comes up. Language Standard 9.11.

**2.10**  The client selects one option for Start and one option for Target. Start is the option that is true of the client today. Target is the option the client intends to reach. Language Standard 3.14.

**2.11**  The Target is the client’s decision. A client who selects a Target of 5 has stated how far they want this subject to go. The agent works to that Target.

**2.12**  A low Start describes the subject today. The agent does not treat a low Start as a judgment of the client. Language Standard 6.14.

## 2.13 The Enrollment report and the outcomes on file
**2.13.1**  The client’s ability is assessed twice. Folder 01 Time, 2.5. The two assessments are kept apart.

**2.13.2  At Enrollment.** The Start and the Target are the client’s own report. The client selects from what they know of themselves. No outcome on file is required before the client selects an option. No option is held back.

**2.13.3  About half and most.** At Enrollment these words are the client’s own estimate of a share. The client is not asked to prove the share. The founder ruled this on October 11, 2026.

**2.13.4  Once Time is operating.** The agent assesses what is happening from the outcomes on file. The agent does not use the Enrollment Start as evidence of what happened.

**2.13.5  The Enrollment Start is kept.** The agent and software never replace it, correct it, or overwrite it. It stays on file with its date. Language Standard 3.16.

**2.13.6**  Where the outcomes on file and the Enrollment Start disagree, both stay on file. The agent works from the outcomes. The agent states the outcomes and does not tell the client that the Start was wrong. Language Standard 6.3.

**2.13.7**  Only the client selects a Start. Section 22 holds the question of whether a client selects a second Start later.

**2.13.8**  Folder 01 Time Research found that two facts cannot be measured before outcomes exist: how close an estimate is, and how often a Plan is carried out. A client still selects those options at Enrollment, as a self report.

# 3. How the answers direct the agent
**3.1**  The answers produce three outputs. Language Standard 3.20.

**3.1.1  Position.** Start minus Target. Position shows where work exists.

**3.1.2  Baseline.** What the client already does and what is already on file. The agent does not request what is already on file.

**3.1.3  Engagement level.** How much the agent asks the client for on one subject.

**3.2**  Two rules of Version 1 are retired. Folder 01 Time, 5.9.

**3.2.1  Lowest position first.** Position does not set the order of work. The agent determines the next action from five factors: the information still missing, the commitments the client has established, the importance of the work to the client, the consequences on file, and the client’s preferences. Language Standard 3.20.2.

**3.2.2  A low Start means more frequent questions.** The column How often it asks is removed from each table. How often the client is contacted follows the client’s contact preferences. Folder 01 Time, 5.4.

**3.3**  Engagement level and contact frequency are two separate settings. Engagement level comes from the twelve answers. Contact frequency comes from the client. A low Start never raises how often the client is contacted.

**3.4**  A need that is urgent or tied to a date is addressed when it arises, whatever its position. Language Standard 3.20.3.

**3.5**  Each question carries a table. The table states where the agent begins at each Start. Once outcomes are on file, the agent works from the outcomes.

**3.6**  Each row is carried out inside three limits: the client’s decisions, the client’s contact preferences, and the standing permissions on file. Where a row and one of the three disagree, the limit governs. Folder 01 Time, 5.4.9, 5.6, and 8.7.

**3.7**  The agent does the same administrative work at every Start. The table changes what the agent asks about. It never reduces the support a client receives, and it never treats support as a lower level.

**3.8**  The agent’s administrative work stays with the agent. The agent does the administrative work the client has authorized, schedules the work that remains, and keeps each open follow up active. Folder 01 Time, 2.4.3.

**3.9**  The client is asked for four kinds of input only: missing information, a decision that belongs to the client, a permission, and an action only the client can do. Folder 01 Time, 5.5.6.

**3.10**  No question, no option, and no lesson asks the client to keep a second copy of what the agent keeps.

**3.11**  Work that the agent or software carried out under a standing permission counts toward the client’s options. A client is never placed at a lower option because the agent did the administrative part.

# 4. How the agent talks with the client
**4.1**  The client answers the twelve questions at Enrollment. Folder 01 Time, 9.2. After Enrollment the agent learns the client’s circumstances through ordinary conversation. Folder 01 Time, 5.1.

**4.2**  The agent asks one question at a time. The agent does not ask for information already on file. Folder 01 Time, 6.2.

**4.3**  Every question has a purpose before it is asked. The purpose is one missing piece. Folder 01 Time, 6.1.

**4.4**  A message is short and holds one thought until the client asks for a different length. The client can ask for more conversation, more explanation, a different pace, or silence. Language Standard 4.5.

**4.5**  The agent acknowledges what the client said before the agent raises a pattern or an analysis. Acknowledgment and natural encouragement are permitted. The agent invents no praise, judges no client, and claims no outcome that has not occurred. Language Standard 10.6.2.

**4.6**  A pattern that is not urgent waits until the client has finished the present subject.

**4.7**  A pattern is stated only from outcomes on file. The agent states the count and the dates. The agent does not state a cause. Language Standard 6.3.

**4.8**  An urgent matter is raised at once. A matter is urgent when it is tied to a date that is near or to a consequence on file.

**4.9**  Where a fact is on file, the agent states it. Where a fact is uncertain, the agent asks.

**4.10**  The five conditions of a commitment are internal requirements. They are never asked as five questions. Folder 01 Time, 3.3.3.

**4.11**  The agent asks about a commitment by name. The client is never asked to reconstruct a whole day from memory. Folder 01 Time, 5.4.7. The Version 1 instructions to reconstruct a night or an evening hour by hour are retired.

**4.12**  The agent does not comment on how the client spends time. The agent does not describe a position, a grade, or a figure from the assessment as good or bad. Folder 01 Time, 8.2. Language Standard 6.14.3.

**4.13**  The client decides what goes on the Plan, what each commitment requires, and what changes. Folder 01 Time, 8.7.

**4.14**  The agent can recommend a practical next action, and can recommend before the client asks. A recommendation is not a decision. The client accepts it, changes it, or declines it. Folder 01 Time, 5.10. Language Standard 10.11.

**4.15**  When the client asks for help or says they cannot start, the agent recommends one step and puts the whole Plan aside. The Plan returns when the client asks for it. Folder 01 Time, 5.13.4.

**4.16**  The agent and the guide adapt wording, warmth, pace, humor, directness, the depth of conversation, and the style of guidance to the client and to the activity. No adaptation changes a fact on file, a permission, the client’s authority, an option, or a score. Language Standard 4.9 to 4.11.

**4.17**  The guide is software. It is identified as software and it is separate from the agent. Each rule in this section applies to the guide. Folder 01 Time, 5.2.

**4.18**  The standard wording of the agent’s questions is held in Folder 01 Time, section 6. This document adds no question to it. The agent and the guide can adapt that wording. The rules in Folder 01 Time, 6.14, hold in every wording.

# 6. Question one: Commitments
**6.1  Measures:** commitments known ahead.

**6.2  Asked as:** How far ahead do you know your commitments?

**6.3  Progression:** How far ahead the client knows a commitment that is already set, and then how current that knowledge is kept. The ten levels in order: 1 learned after its time has passed; 2 learned on the day it is due; 3 today known at the start of the day; 4 tomorrow known; 5 three days known; 6 seven days known; 7 seven days known with times and places; 8 known past seven days; 9 a new or changed commitment added the day it is set; 10 known ahead for a month, with a late arrival added when it arrived.

## 6.4 Start options
The client selects the one that is true of them today.

**1.  I find out about a commitment after its time has passed.**

You learn of a commitment once it is over.

**2.  I find out about a commitment on the day it is due.**

You learn of it that day.

**3.  At the start of my day, I can say what is set for today.**

Today is known when it starts.

**4.  I can say what is set for tomorrow.**

One day ahead is known.

**5.  I can say what is set for the next three days.**

Three days ahead are known.

**6.  I can say what is set for the next seven days.**

A week ahead is known.

**7.  I can say the time and the place of what is set for the next seven days.**

The week is known with its times and places.

**8.  I can say what is set past the next seven days.**

Commitments further out are known.

**9.  A new or changed commitment is added to the place I keep my commitments on the day it is set.**

What you know stays current.

**10.  For the past month I knew my commitments ahead of time. One that arrived late was added when it arrived.**

Knowing your commitments has worked for a month.

## 6.5 Target options
Each completes the phrase my target is to. The client selects the one they are aiming for.

**1.**  Deal with a commitment when it comes up.

**2.**  Find out about a commitment on the day it is due.

**3.**  Be able to say at the start of my day what is set for today.

**4.**  Be able to say what is set for tomorrow.

**5.**  Be able to say what is set for the next three days.

**6.**  Be able to say what is set for the next seven days.

**7.**  Be able to say the time and the place of what is set for the next seven days.

**8.**  Be able to say what is set past the next seven days.

**9.**  Have a new or changed commitment added on the day it is set.

**10.**  Know my commitments ahead of time for a month, and have one that arrives late added when it arrives.

# 7. Question two: Taking on a commitment
**7.1  Measures:** taking on new commitments.

**7.2  Asked as:** When you take on a new commitment, what do you find out about your time?

**7.3  Progression:** How much of the client’s time is checked when a new commitment is taken on, and then whether a small commitment gets the same check. The ten levels in order: 1 no check; 2 a check afterward; 3 the hour; 4 the day; 5 the week; 6 the full time of the commitment; 7 what it moves or replaces; 8 what changes decided the same day; 9 the same check for a small commitment; 10 the effect known and the change decided for a month.

## 7.4 Start options
The client selects the one that is true of them today.

**1.  I take on a new commitment and do not look at my time.**

No check happens.

**2.  I look at my time after I have taken on a new commitment.**

The check comes afterward.

**3.  When I take on a new commitment, I find out whether that hour is already taken.**

The hour is checked.

**4.  When I take on a new commitment, I find out what else is set for that day.**

The day is checked.

**5.  When I take on a new commitment, I find out what else is set for that week.**

The week is checked.

**6.  When I take on a new commitment, I find out its full time: getting ready, travel, and the time I need after it.**

The time around it is known.

**7.  When I take on a new commitment, I can say what it moves or replaces.**

You know what it takes time from.

**8.  When a new commitment does not fit, I decide what changes on the day I take it on.**

The decision is made that day.

**9.  My small commitments get the same check as my large ones.**

A small commitment gets the same check.

**10.  For the past month I knew what a new commitment took from my time when I took it on. I decided what changed when one did not fit.**

Taking on commitments has worked for a month.

## 7.5 Target options
Each completes the phrase my target is to. The client selects the one they are aiming for.

**1.**  Deal with a new commitment when its day comes.

**2.**  Look at my time after taking on a new commitment.

**3.**  Find out whether the hour is already taken when taking on a new commitment.

**4.**  Find out what else is set for that day when taking on a new commitment.

**5.**  Find out what else is set for that week when taking on a new commitment.

**6.**  Find out the full time of a new commitment when taking it on.

**7.**  Be able to say what a new commitment moves or replaces.

**8.**  Decide what changes on the day a commitment that does not fit is taken on.

**9.**  Have my small commitments get the same check as my large ones.

**10.**  Know for a month what a new commitment takes from my time, and decide what changes when one does not fit.

# 9. Question four: Time a commitment takes
**9.1  Measures:** a commitment’s full time.

**9.2  Asked as:** How well do you know how long a commitment takes you in full?

**9.3  Progression:** How much of the full time of a commitment the client knows, and then whether the figure comes from what happened and is kept current. The ten levels in order: 1 no figure; 2 the set length; 3 the time before; 4 the time after; 5 the full time of one commitment from a day it happened; 6 the full time of the commitments that come back; 7 the commitments whose time differs from the time allowed; 8 the plan uses the time taken before; 9 a different time reported or corrected the same day; 10 planned time close to the time taken for a month, with a wrong figure corrected.

## 9.4 Start options
The client selects the one that is true of them today.

**1.  I cannot say how long my commitments take.**

No figure exists.

**2.  I can say how long a commitment is set to last.**

The set length is known.

**3.  I can say the time I need before a commitment: getting ready and getting there.**

The time before is known.

**4.  I can say the time I need after a commitment: getting back and the time before I can start the next one.**

The time after is known.

**5.  I can say the full time one commitment took on a day it happened.**

One figure comes from what happened.

## 19.20 Progress that is written down and reported
**19.20.1  What it says.** Checking progress on a commitment improved results in research. The effect was larger when the progress was written down and when it was reported to another person.

**19.20.2  When it is taught.** The time of Daily Close is being set, or the client asks why the agent asks what happened.

**19.20.3  From the client’s file.** The agent states which commitments on the past seven Plans have an outcome on file and which have none.

**19.20.4  Where it comes from.** Harkin and others reviewed 138 randomized studies with 19,951 people. A prompt to monitor progress improved the outcome, d \= 0.40. After the authors adjusted for unpublished studies the effect was d \= 0.19. The effect was d \= 0.47 when progress was reported to another person and d \= 0.19 when it was kept private. It was d \= 0.43 when progress was written down and d \= 0.29 when it was not. All but one of the studies concerned health.

**19.20.5  Daily Close.** Daily Close is a program design that follows from this finding. No study has tested Daily Close. The agent does not describe Daily Close to a client as tested.

**19.20.6  Kind of support.** Supports the practice of monitoring progress. Daily Close is a design inference.

Source: [Harkin and others, Does monitoring goal progress promote goal attainment?, accepted manuscript, 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/)

**19.22.2  When it is taught.** A recurring responsibility is being set up, or the client missed one.

**19.22.3  From the client’s file.** The agent states the recurrence the client stated and the dates the responsibility was done. After a miss, the agent states the date of the next one.

**19.22.4  Where it comes from.** Lally and others followed 96 volunteers who each repeated one daily action for 12 weeks. The time for the action to become automatic ran from 18 to 254 days. Missing one day did not change the result. The study covered one small daily action. It is applied to a recurring responsibility by inference.

**19.22.5  Kind of support.** Analogy.

Source: [Lally and others, How are habits formed, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)

## 19.23 A reminder is support
**19.23.1  What it says.** A reminder, a schedule, a list, a calendar, an agent, Guided Life, and a person who helps are support. Using support does not lower an assessment. Knowing which commitments need support is an ability.

**19.23.2  When it is taught.** The client holds back from asking for a reminder, or a commitment was missed and no reminder was set for it.

**19.23.3  From the client’s file.** The agent states which commitments on the Plan have a reminder set and which have none. The agent offers one reminder.

**19.23.4  Where it comes from.** In a Cochrane review, attendance at health appointments was 67.8 percent with no reminder and 78.6 percent with a text reminder. In a review of 16 trials with 2,742 patients, text messages raised the odds of taking medication as prescribed, with an odds ratio of 2.11. Both reviews cover health care.

**19.23.5  Two further sources.** Gilbert and others found that people set more reminders than was best for them in a memory task of about one minute. That finding is an analogy. The World Health Organization classification keeps apart what a person does with no assistance and what a person does with the devices and the people who assist them. That distinction is a framework.

**19.23.6  Kind of support.** Supports the practice for a reminder before a commitment. The rule that support does not lower an assessment is a program position.

Source: [Cochrane review, Mobile phone messaging reminders for attendance at healthcare appointments, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007458.pub3)

Source: [Thakkar and others, Mobile telephone text messaging for medication adherence in chronic disease, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.7667)

Source: [Gilbert and others, Optimal use of reminders, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000652)

Source: [World Health Organization, Towards a Common Language for Functioning, Disability and Health, 2002](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/classification/icf/icfbeginnersguide.pdf)

**20.11**  No lesson compares the client with a second client or with an average. Language Standard 6.3.3.
