Department I, Agency Assessment. Internal specification. Version 4.1, October 11, 2026. What the folder is, what goes on file, and how the agent runs it.

## 2. What it is

2.1 **Name.** Time.

2.2 **Descriptor.** Planning your commitments and following through on each one.

2.3 **Definition.** Time covers how a client plans commitments, executes them, reviews what happened, and follows through on the work that remains. The agent establishes what has to happen, places it in the schedule within the client's circumstances, checks what happened, and keeps the remaining work on the schedule until its requirements are met. Time measures the client's ability to plan, execute, and review commitments under the conditions the client has set. Time does not measure how much a client does.

2.4 The folder delivers five results, in this order.

2.4.1 A schedule the client did not have to build. The agent assembles it from what the client says in conversation. A client can answer what time they got up. A client is never asked to fill an empty grid. The resident can view the calendar as intake begins and see established commitments take their places as the schedule is assembled.

2.4.2 A Plan for each day. The Plan lists the commitments the client intends to execute that day.

2.4.3 Follow through that the agent carries out. After a commitment is executed, the agent does the administrative work the client has authorized, schedules the work that remains, and keeps each open follow up active.

2.4.4 A history of what happened to each commitment. The agent and the client use it to make the next Plan one the client can execute.

2.4.5 Two figures from the schedule. The hours a week the client controls, and the longest unbroken block in a normal week.

2.5 The client's ability to plan and execute is assessed twice. The first assessment is the Start and Target the client selects at Enrollment. The second is the outcomes that go on file during residency.

2.6 Each outcome is compared with the conditions that client stated. Time holds no punctuality rule that applies to every client.

2.7 A commitment to rest counts the same as every other commitment. A Plan is never graded by how many commitments it holds.

2.8 Time works with two questions. Did today go as planned is the question of Daily Close. How many hours a week are mine, and where do they sit is the question the schedule answers. The folder list keeps the second one as the question for Time.

2.9 Conversation is how the schedule gets built from nothing. The client can also edit the schedule by hand at any point. A client who adds and moves blocks by hand is never routed through the agent to do it.

2.9.1 The schedule is visible as a calendar in day, week, and month views. The day and week views place known timed blocks at their actual times. The month view places dated entries on their dates. These views read and change the same maintained schedule; they are not separate calendars or copies of commitments.

2.9.2 A calendar may display incomplete information without treating it as complete. A dated commitment whose start or end is not established remains identifiable, with its missing time visible; the software does not invent a start, end, or duration to fill a slot. A proposed or tentative time remains distinct from confirmed time under the approval rules.

2.10 A block typed in by hand and a block built from a client's statement are the same once filed.

### 2.11 Client copy, folder description

> Time
>
> You do not have to build a schedule. You tell your agent what you need to do, and your agent builds the schedule from what you say.
>
> You and your agent make a plan for each day. Your agent asks what happened, updates your information where you have given permission, and puts the work that is left on your schedule.
>
> Your schedule shows how many hours a week are yours and where they sit.
>
> Did today go as planned?

## 3. What goes on file

3.1 Four kinds of entry go on file in this folder: a block, a commitment, a follow up, and ongoing work.

### 3.2 The block

3.2.1 A **block** is a period of time with a start, an end, and a label. Each block also carries the four fields below.

3.2.2 **Observed or estimated.** Observed means it happened and the client knows it. Estimated means it is a guess, a usual amount, or a period that has not yet taken place.

3.2.3 **Paid, unpaid, or neither.** Paid means the client is compensated for those hours. Unpaid means the time is required by the work and is not paid for. Travel to a shift is unpaid time. Neither means the block is unrelated to work.

3.2.4 **Cost of.** Which commitment the block exists because of, when the block is not a commitment itself. Travel, preparation, and recovery are costs of the commitment they surround.

3.2.5 **Planned or done.**

### 3.3 The commitment

3.3.1 A **commitment** is a specific action the client intends to execute. **Execution** is the carrying out of a commitment. Each commitment has a label and the block it is planned in.

3.3.2 A commitment carries five conditions. The agent fills a condition only where it is relevant to that commitment. A condition the client has not stated stays unknown.

| Condition | What goes on file |
| :-- | :-- |
| Timing | The required date and time, the deadline, the duration, and how far the timing can move |
| Completion | What the client says must happen for the commitment to be fulfilled |
| Consequences | What the client says a delay, a change, or a miss would affect. Separately, what the client later reports did happen |
| Standards | Which minimums from the client's Standards folder apply |
| Follow through | Each action required after the commitment is executed, with its owner, its timing, and the ongoing work it belongs to |

3.3.3 The five conditions are internal requirements. They are not a script of five questions. A client can state more than one condition in one sentence.

3.3.4 A commitment can go on a Plan when three facts are on file: the action, the client's authorization or an agreement already on file, and a date or time that is enough to schedule it. Each other condition is collected when it is relevant.

3.3.5 **Outcome.** What happened to a commitment is held in four separate fields. No single label carries more than one of them.

3.3.6 **Execution.** One of four values: planned, executed, partly executed, or did not happen. Execution is measured against the Completion condition the client stated.

3.3.7 **Timing.** One of three values: as planned, changed, or not established. Timing is filed as changed when the actual time differs from the Plan. Where the client reported the time, the actual time is on file.

3.3.8 **Consequences.** One of three values: reported, none confirmed, or not established. Reported means the client said what was affected, and each consequence is on file as its own entry. None confirmed means the client said no commitment or condition was affected. Not established means no information about consequences is on file.

3.3.9 Not established is never read as none confirmed. The absence of a report does not prove that no harm happened.

3.3.10 **Alternative.** Whether the client used an alternative that was agreed before the commitment was due, and which one.

3.3.11 A changed time says nothing about execution. A commitment can be executed with changed timing and no consequence.

3.3.12 Executing an action and finishing the larger responsibility are two separate facts. The outcome of a commitment is held apart from the status of its follow ups and of the ongoing work it belongs to. A commitment stays executed while linked work is open.

### 3.4 The follow up

3.4.1 A **follow up** is information, a confirmation, a client action, or an agent action that is still required after a conversation or a commitment.

3.4.2 Each follow up carries four fields: its owner, its next action, the conversation or commitment it came from, and a status of open or closed. The owner is the client or the agent.

### 3.5 Ongoing work

3.5.1 **Ongoing work** is a continuing responsibility that contains more than one commitment or follow up. It can recur.

3.5.2 Ongoing work carries a label, the recurrence the client has stated, and a link to each commitment and follow up inside it.

3.5.3 One case. Laundry is ongoing work. Washing, drying, and putting clothes away are three commitments inside it. Washing can be executed while putting clothes away is still planned. The laundry stays active and Time schedules the remaining commitment.

### 3.6 Rules governing the record

3.6.1 A period in the future is estimated. No planned block is ever filed as observed.

3.6.2 An estimate becomes observed only when the client says what happened. The agent does not promote it and does not round a figure into a fact.

3.6.3 The hours a client controls are calculated from observed time only. Estimates stay on file and are excluded from the figure.

3.6.4 No entry is invented. A statement the client did not make is not on file. Where a statement is unclear the agent asks.

3.6.5 An expected consequence is never filed as a consequence that happened. The two are separate fields.

3.6.6 A commitment is marked executed only when the client reports it or evidence on file proves it. A planned block does not prove that the commitment happened.

3.6.7 Planned, estimated, and observed entries stay distinguishable in every view.

3.6.8 Every entry keeps who wrote it: the client, the agent, or software.

## 4. Routing

4.1 This folder holds hours, commitments, and the work that follows a commitment. It does not hold the subject the hours were spent on. Where both are relevant, the hours and the commitment are filed here and the subject is filed in the folder named below.

| What arrives | Filed in Time | Also filed in |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| A shift | The hours worked | Career Development, Schedule, as the shift entry |
| Travel to a shift | The hours, as a cost of that shift | Nothing. Travel is not its own commitment |
| A vehicle, fare, or breakdown | Nothing | Housing Stability, Transportation |
| Sleep | The hours | Nothing |
| Insomnia or a sleep problem | The hours only | Agency Assessment, Health |
| A medical appointment | The hours, with travel attached | Agency Assessment, Health, as the care |
| A court or government appointment | The hours, with travel and waiting | Legal or Administration, as the matter |
| Caring for a person | The hours and the relationship | Agency Assessment, Background, as the person |
| Housework | The hours | Sanitation or Organization, as the task |
| Seeing friends | The hours | Life Management, Socializing, as the relationship |
| An evening spent on screens | The hours | Agency Assessment, Regulation, as what it did or did not restore |
| Work on the client's own project | The hours | The relevant Career or Education folder |
| Pay for hours worked | Nothing | Career Development, Salary |

4.2 A block filed here with no hours attached is non compliant. If the client reported a subject and no time, it belongs in the other folder alone.

4.3 A commitment with no time set stays on file with Timing unknown. It receives a block when the timing is established.

### 4.4 Coordination across folders

4.4.1 Work that starts in another folder is scheduled here. When a folder produces an action with a time, a deadline, or a recurrence, the action is placed in the schedule as a commitment linked to that folder.

4.4.2 An executed commitment can require a change in another folder. The agent prepares each change in the folder it belongs to. Time keeps the link between the commitment and the change.

4.4.3 Career Development, Schedule. The work schedule supplies planned shifts. Time holds what was worked.

4.4.4 Life Management, Standards. Standards holds the client's minimums. Time holds which minimums apply to a commitment. Time never sets a minimum.

4.4.5 Housing Stability, Inventory. Where the items from an executed purchase are known and Inventory maintains them, the Inventory changes are prepared in Inventory.

4.4.6 Agency Assessment, Finance. A purchase amount from an executed commitment is filed in Finance. Salary holds no second copy of it.

4.4.7 Career Development, Integrity. Integrity covers every commitment the client made, at work and outside work. Integrity reads the commitments already on file in Time. Integrity holds no second schedule and no second copy of a commitment.

4.4.8 Time holds when a commitment is scheduled and what happened to it. Integrity holds the client's record of whether they did what they said they would do.

## 5. How it works

5.1 The agent runs the folder. The agent learns the client's circumstances through ordinary conversation. The agent establishes what has to happen, places it in the schedule, checks what happened, follows up, and keeps the remaining work active. The client reports their life, makes the decisions that belong to them, and carries out the actions only the client can do.

5.2 **Agent and software.** The agent is the person who represents the client and answers for the work. Software carries out defined work under rules and permissions, on the agent's behalf. Software is never called the agent and never presents as a person. Language Standard 3.11 and 10.8.1.

### 5.3 Planning

5.3.1 A **Plan** is the list of commitments the client intends to execute on one day. The agent presents the commitments already known for that day and asks what is different from usual. The client confirms the Plan.

5.3.2 The **schedule** is the recurring structure of the client's week. The agent maintains it from repeated Plans, outcomes, and client statements. A stated schedule does not override repeated outcomes that show the week runs differently.

5.3.3 Before the first Plan is saved, the agent asks how realistic it feels to the client. A Plan the client calls unlikely is simplified or moved before it is saved.

5.3.4 Time with Universe City is itself placed on the Plan. The first Plan asks what time Daily Close belongs in the client's day.

### 5.4 Contact

5.4.1 Contact has three origins.

| Origin | What starts it |
| :-- | :-- |
| Client requested | The client asks for contact or for an action |
| Permitted automatic | A standing permission already covers the action. Software carries it out and notifies the client |
| Agent initiated | The agent decides that contact or intervention is needed |

5.4.2 Contact takes place at three points in the day: the Morning Plan, during the day, and Daily Close. Daily Close is one of three kinds of daily contact.

5.4.3 **Morning Plan.** An optional message at the start of the day. It states the day's commitments, their timing, and each item that needs the client's attention.

5.4.4 **During the day.** Four kinds of message: a reminder before a commitment, an update when a Plan or the schedule changes, a notice that an action was carried out, and a follow up when further work needs the client.

5.4.5 **Daily Close** is the daily contact in which the agent and the client review what happened, address the remaining work, and confirm the next day's Plan. It is the regular contact.

5.4.6 Daily Close opens with the first question in 6.5. The agent then asks about a commitment only where 7.15 applies.

5.4.7 The agent asks about the commitment by name. The client is never asked to reconstruct the whole day from memory.

5.4.8 The client does not have to wait for Daily Close. The client can report a new commitment, a change, or an outcome at any time, and it is filed the same way.

5.4.9 The client chooses how much contact they receive and when. The client can turn on the Morning Plan, reminders, updates, and notices separately.

5.4.10 Contact is managed by five factors: the client's contact preferences, the timing and importance of the commitment, the consequences and minimums on file for it, whether information, a decision, or an action is needed from the client, and whether an authorized action was carried out and the client asked to be told.

5.4.11 A message states information the client can use. A message asks for a reply only when information, a decision, or an action is needed from the client. Routine authorized work proceeds with no reply.

5.4.12 **Sender.** A message the agent wrote or released is shown as from the agent. A message that software sent under a standing permission is shown as an automated Universe City message. Software never signs as the agent.

5.4.13 The contact settings, the delivery methods, and the scheduling rules are specified in Folder 01 Time Operations.

### 5.5 Follow through

5.5.1 When the client reports an executed commitment, four steps run in order.

5.5.2 One. The commitment is marked executed, with the time and each consequence the client reported.

5.5.3 Two. The Follow through condition, the linked ongoing work, and the requirements of each folder the commitment is linked to are checked.

5.5.4 Three. Each piece of remaining work a standing permission covers is carried out and filed. No reply is requested from the client. Each other piece is prepared for the agent.

5.5.5 Four. A follow up is opened for each piece of information, each decision, and each permission that is still missing.

5.5.6 The client is asked for four kinds of input only: missing information, a decision that belongs to the client, a permission, and an action only the client can do.

5.5.7 No work is created to make the client interact. A receipt, an amount, or an itemized list is requested only when the folder that receives it needs it.

### 5.6 Authorization

5.6.1 Three acts are kept separate: preparation, authorized execution, and approval of a permanent change to the client's file. One never stands in for another.

5.6.2 **Preparation.** The agent or software drafts an update, a schedule change, or a message. No entry on file changes. Preparation needs no permission.

5.6.3 **Authorized execution.** A **standing permission** is a permission the client gave in advance for a named kind of routine action. An action it covers is carried out with no further approval. Software can carry it out with no person releasing it.

5.6.4 An action carried out under a standing permission is filed with who carried it out and which permission covered it.

5.6.5 **Approval of a permanent change.** An entry that states what the client directly reported is filed as the client's statement. An entry that required interpretation is a proposal. A proposal is filed only under the proposal and approval rules in `ARCHITECTURE.md`. No standing permission covers a proposal. Software never approves one.

5.6.6 A person is required in four cases: the permission does not clearly cover the action, judgment is needed beyond the established rule, the action would change the client's file or obligations in a way that requires interpretation, or the client has asked for review by a person.

5.6.7 The client's decision is required in six cases: a commitment the client has not agreed to, two commitments that compete for the same time, a change to a minimum in Standards, a change that cannot be reversed, a record to be voided, and a message to be sent on the client's behalf. Language Standard 10.9.1.

5.6.8 Each standing permission and the actions it covers are specified in Folder 01 Time Operations.

### 5.7 One case, grocery shopping

5.7.1 The client reports that the grocery shopping happened.

5.7.2 The shopping commitment is marked executed.

5.7.3 Where a purchase amount is available and Finance uses it, the Finance entry is prepared.

5.7.4 Where the items are known and Inventory maintains them, the Inventory changes are prepared.

5.7.5 The remaining work is identified. The part a standing permission covers is placed on the schedule.

5.7.6 Each unresolved follow up stays open until its requirement is met.

5.7.7 Steps 5.7.2 to 5.7.6 are done by the agent and by software. The client takes part only where information, a permission, a decision, or an action only the client can do is required. The shopping commitment stays executed while linked work is open.

### 5.8 The schedule by hand

5.8.1 The client can open the schedule and work on it directly. Blocks can be dragged, resized, added, edited, and deleted. Every change is kept on file and the agent can see what changed.

### 5.9 Order and frequency

5.9.1 The Version 3 rule that set the order of work by lowest position first is retired.

5.9.2 The agent selects the next question by four factors: the information still missing, the importance of the commitment to the client, the consequences on file, and the client's preferences.

5.9.3 The Version 3 rule that set how often the agent asks by the twelve answers is retired. How often the client is contacted is governed by 5.4.

5.9.4 Start, Target, and Position for Time still come from the twelve questions. Position does not set the order of work in Time.

## 6. The agent's questions

6.1 Every question has a purpose before it is asked. The purpose is one missing piece: a block, a condition of a commitment, an outcome, a consequence, a decision, or a permission. A question that names no missing piece is not asked.

6.2 The agent asks one question at a time. The agent does not ask for information already on file.

6.3 The wording below is what the agent uses. The questions use grocery shopping as the subject. The agent substitutes the actual subject every time.

### 6.4 Making a Plan

> What already has to happen tomorrow?
>
> Is there a part of tomorrow you want to be different from usual?
>
> Here is what I have for tomorrow. Is that still the plan?
>
> How realistic does this plan feel to you?
>
> What time tomorrow do you want to talk about how the day went?

### 6.5 Checking an outcome

> Did today go as planned?
>
> Did the grocery shopping happen?
>
> Is the grocery shopping finished?
>
> What time did the grocery shopping happen?
>
> Did the later time affect another commitment?
>
> Is the grocery shopping still needed?

### 6.6 Establishing a condition

> What time does the grocery shopping need to happen?
>
> How late can the grocery shopping move?
>
> What has to happen for the grocery shopping to count as done?
>
> What does it affect if the grocery shopping moves or does not happen?
>
> Does one of your minimums apply to the grocery shopping?
>
> What is left to do after the grocery shopping?

### 6.7 Asking for a decision or a permission

> The grocery shopping and your appointment are both at 5:00 PM. Which one holds?
>
> Do you want me to move the grocery shopping to tomorrow at 10:00 AM?
>
> Do you want me to put the remaining work on your schedule when the time is already known?

### 6.8 Setting contact

> Do you want a message each morning with the plan for the day?
>
> Do you want a reminder before the grocery shopping?
>
> Do you want a notice each time I update your schedule?

### 6.9 Collecting a block

6.9.1 These questions are used when a block is missing. They are not asked as a fixed sequence.

> What time did you wake up?
>
> How long did getting ready take you this morning?
>
> What time did you leave the house?
>
> What time did you actually get to work?
>
> What time did your shift end?
>
> What time did you get home?
>
> How long was it before you started your next activity?

### 6.10 Establishing the kind of block

> Is that shift paid?
>
> Was that required by the job, or did you choose to do it?
>
> Was that part of the shift, or separate from it?

### 6.11 Confidence

> Is that what actually happened yesterday, or roughly what usually happens?
>
> Do you know the actual time, or is that an estimate?

### 6.12 Filling a gap

> What were you doing between 2:00 PM and 5:00 PM?
>
> There is nothing on file for Sunday. What were you doing?

### 6.13 Asked once per new kind of work

> Is that work you get paid for, or your own?
>
> How long does getting ready usually take you?

### 6.14 Rules for every question

6.14.1 Every question names the subject it is asking about. The agent never uses a pronoun where the subject belongs. How long does getting ready usually take you is a question. How long does that usually take you is not ready.

6.14.2 A question that could be answered about more than one subject is not ready. What time did you finish is not a question. What time did your shift end is.

6.14.3 The agent treats a client's direct statement as a statement. The agent does not repeat it back and ask whether the client meant it. The agent asks for clarification in two cases: the answer is ambiguous, or a consequential change to the file depends on it.

6.14.4 Every time in a question is written with AM or PM.

6.14.5 The agent does not ask what time it is or what day it is. The current date and time are always available to it.

6.14.6 The agent speaks times as 7:00 AM. It never uses twenty four hour time.

## 7. Triggers

7.1 The conditions under which a subject is raised that the client did not bring up.

7.2 No conversation is opened for work that needs no input from the client. An action a standing permission covers is carried out and filed. The client is notified where the client's contact preferences ask for a notice.

7.3 A commitment is reported executed. The four steps in 5.5 run.

7.4 A commitment did not happen or was partly executed. The agent asks whether the remaining part is still required. The agent keeps it, moves it, or removes it as the client decides.

7.5 A commitment is executed and its follow through is open. The work a standing permission covers is scheduled with no question to the client. The agent asks only for a missing piece.

7.6 A recurring responsibility comes due. The next commitment is placed on the schedule under the recurrence the client stated. No question is asked.

7.7 A commitment is added that overlaps an existing block. The agent states the conflict and asks which one holds.

7.8 A change affects a commitment that has a stated consequence or an applicable minimum. The agent states the effect and asks the client to decide.

7.9 A commitment is added with no travel attached. The agent asks how the client is getting there and back.

7.10 A block is edited by hand. The agent can see the change and may ask about it. It does not treat the change as a correction to be logged separately.

7.11 A day passes with no entry on file. The agent asks about that day only where an open requirement depends on it. The three requirements are a commitment on that day's Plan with no outcome, an open follow up, and a block the schedule is still missing. The agent asks once, at the next contact. Where no requirement is open, the agent does not ask.

7.12 An estimate has stood for more than a week. The agent asks whether the client now knows the actual figure.

7.13 A commitment is added that takes the controlled hours figure below the client's stated target. The agent states the figure before and after. It does not advise.

7.14 A block the client claimed for their own work is repeatedly given to another commitment. The agent states how many times it has happened.

7.15 The agent does not ask about every commitment on a Plan. A planned commitment gets a question at Daily Close when one of these seven is true: it is new, the client marked it important, its timing is uncertain, it has differed from the Plan more than once, another commitment depends on it, the client asked for accountability on it, or it is a change from the client's usual week.

7.16 The seven conditions rest on five factors: how uncertain the commitment is, how important it is to the client, the consequences on file for it, the outcomes already on file, and the client's preference.

7.17 The agent asks less about a commitment as its outcomes keep matching the Plan. No fixed count of matching outcomes applies. The criteria are specified in Folder 01 Time Operations.

## 8. Folder limits

8.1 These apply in addition to section 10 of the Language Standard.

8.2 The agent does not comment on how the client spends their time. It files the hours and the outcomes.

8.3 The agent does not tell a client they are working too much, sleeping too little, or spending too long on a subject.

8.4 The agent does not interpret sleep, fatigue, or a condition the client describes as a medical problem. Those are filed as hours and routed to Health.

8.5 The agent does not calculate a real hourly rate. That is Salary, and it depends on this folder.

8.6 The agent does not fill a gap in the record with a typical day. An unaccounted hour stays unaccounted until the client says otherwise.

8.7 **Client authority.** The client decides what goes on the Plan, what each commitment requires, and what changes. The agent does not remove a commitment, change a condition, or set a minimum without the client's decision. The client can edit the schedule at any time.

8.8 **Authorized action.** The agent and software take only the actions the client has permitted. A standing permission never covers a record to be voided, a message sent on the client's behalf, or a change the client cannot reverse. Each of the three needs the client's confirmation. Language Standard 10.9.1.

8.9 **Evidence.** A source proves only what it directly proves. A planned block does not prove the commitment happened. A bank transaction does not prove why the money was spent. Silence does not prove a commitment was missed.

8.10 The agent does not invent a consequence, an amount, a purchase, or a completion.

8.11 The agent does not treat a changed or missed commitment as a failure. The outcome is filed and the next Plan uses it. The agent does not comment on the client's reliability or effort. Language Standard 10.6.4.

## 9. The arc

9.1 Three stages: intake, analyze and adapt, system maintenance. Language Standard 3.7 and 8.2.11. The four stage arc of Version 3 is superseded.

9.2 **Before the arc.** Enrollment establishes the client's initial information and the Start and Target for Time. Orientation follows in the Resident Portal. The client does not build the schedule during Enrollment. Initial Time work starts after Orientation. The order is Application, email confirmation, Enrollment, Orientation, Time. The free first day of Version 3 is retired.

9.3 **One, intake.** The agent establishes what already has the client's time, assembles the first seven day schedule, and makes the first Plan with the client. The first Plan includes the realism check in 5.3.3 and the time of Daily Close in 5.3.4. The Resident Portal home opens when the first seven day schedule is on file and the client has confirmed the first Plan. A completed week of activity is not required.

9.3.1 At the start of Time intake, after Orientation, the calendar is available even if the schedule is not yet established. The agent fills it from existing File information and new conversation as timing becomes established and authorized. An unfilled portion does not prevent the client from seeing or using the calendar. The conditions in 9.3 and section 10 still govern when the initial system is operational; calendar visibility does not certify that those conditions have been met.

9.4 **Two, analyze and adapt.** The client carries out the Plan. Outcomes are checked at Daily Close, follow through is carried out under 5.5, and the agent compares each Plan with what happened. Where the same outcome repeats, the agent proposes a change to the next Plan or to the schedule. The client decides.

9.5 **Three, system maintenance.** The agent asks fewer questions as commitments become established. Contact follows the client's preferences in 5.4. The agent asks about what is missing, what changed, and the commitments named in 7.15. The schedule receives commitments from every folder. The Review between the client and the agent uses the outcomes on file.

## 10. Operational conditions

10.1 The folder is operational when all three are true. The folder is never complete. It is established, maintained, and corrected.

10.2 A seven day schedule is on file. It holds the client's recurring commitments, with their travel and preparation filed as costs of them.

10.3 The client has confirmed at least one Plan.

10.4 The time of Daily Close is on the schedule.

10.5 Two further facts show the system is in use: a completed Daily Close with outcomes on file, and a decision the client answered with the schedule. Neither one is a condition.

10.6 The hours the client controls and the longest unbroken block are figures the schedule produces. Neither one is a condition.

10.7 No follow up is required for the folder to be operational. A client whose commitments produce no further work meets the three conditions.

10.8 The grade measures the work done during the month. It does not measure the target chosen or whether the target was reached.

## 11. The system

11.1 A seven day schedule.

11.2 The schedule is the working system. Eight parts run on it.

| Part | What it is | How it connects |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Schedule | The recurring structure of the client's week | The source of each day's Plan. Changed when repeated outcomes show the week runs differently |
| Plan | The commitments the client intends to execute on one day | Taken from the schedule, plus what is different that day. Confirmed by the client |
| Commitment | A specific action the client intends to execute | Sits on a Plan, in a block. Carries the five conditions and its outcome |
| Follow up | Information, a confirmation, or an action still required | Linked to the conversation or commitment it came from. Open until its requirement is met |
| Ongoing work | A continuing responsibility | Holds its commitments and follow ups. Active through each recurrence |
| Morning Plan | An optional message at the start of the day | Taken from the day's Plan. Sent where the client turned it on |
| Daily Close | The regular daily contact on the day's Plan | Puts the outcome of each commitment on file. Confirms the next Plan |
| Review | The meeting between the client and the agent | Uses the outcomes on file to decide changes to the schedule |

11.3 One commitment can produce more than one follow up. One piece of ongoing work can hold more than one commitment. One action can affect more than one folder. Each link is kept on file, and each change of state is kept on file.

11.4 The schedule is also the client's calendar. Appointments, reminders, and dates from every other folder are placed in it. Time owns the calendar for the whole program.

11.4.1 The calendar is the visible working surface of the schedule. Its day and week views show timed commitments and blocks in time slots. Its month view shows them on their dates. The client can open an entry to see the maintained information, what remains unknown, and its history.

11.4.2 A change established through conversation appears in the calendar. A permitted direct change in the calendar updates the same File entry. The views do not independently own or duplicate the record. They must respect the resident's decisions, authorization, original statements, earlier versions, and links to other folders.

11.5 The schedule produces two figures once a week of observed time is on file: the hours the client controls, and the longest unbroken block.

### 11.6 Successful execution

11.6.1 Two results are kept apart: executed, and confirmed successful.

11.6.2 A commitment is executed when the Completion condition the client stated is met.

11.6.3 Execution is confirmed successful when the commitment is executed and Consequences is none confirmed. Confirmed successful is read from those two fields. It is not a fifth outcome field.

11.6.4 Where Consequences is not established, the commitment is executed and success is not confirmed. Not established is never counted as no harm.

11.6.5 A changed time is filed whenever the actual time differs from the Plan. A changed time alone is never a failure.

11.6.6 A partly executed commitment stays on file as partly executed. It is never filed as executed.

11.6.7 An alternative agreed in advance is executed when it meets the Completion condition. It is confirmed successful on the same terms as every other commitment.

11.6.8 One case. A client plans to buy groceries at 5:00 PM.

| What happened | Execution | Timing | Consequences | Result |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Shopping happened at 5:00 PM | Executed | As planned | Not established | Executed. Success not confirmed |
| Shopping happened at 7:00 PM. The client said no other commitment was affected | Executed | Changed | None confirmed | Confirmed successful |
| Shopping happened at 7:00 PM. The client missed an appointment | Executed | Changed | Reported | Executed, with one consequence on file |
| Shopping was started and was not finished | Partly executed | Not established | Not established | Partly executed |
| Shopping did not happen | Did not happen | Does not apply | Not established | Did not happen |

11.6.9 In row three the agent raises the consequence at the next Review.

11.6.10 In rows four and five the agent asks whether the remaining shopping is still required, then keeps or revises the Plan as the client decides.

11.6.11 The agent does not ask about consequences only to confirm success. The agent asks where the client stated a consequence in advance, or where the timing changed and the new time overlaps a second commitment. In every other case the commitment stays executed with Consequences not established.

11.7 How the system runs is specified in Folder 01 Time Operations.

## 13. Connections

13.1 Serves every folder. Appointments and reminders from every folder are placed in this folder's calendar. An action from another folder that has a time, a deadline, or a recurrence is scheduled here as a commitment.

13.2 Coordinates with Inventory. A shopping commitment executed here produces Inventory changes where the items are known and Inventory maintains them. Work that Inventory produces with a date is scheduled here.

13.3 Feeds Salary. A real hourly rate cannot be calculated until unpaid travel, preparation, and recovery are counted against pay. Salary depends on this folder.

13.4 Fed by Standards. The minimums that apply to a commitment come from Standards.

13.5 Feeds Standards. A decision is checked against both what the client will accept and whether the time exists.

13.6 Feeds Discernment. A commitment that took the controlled hours figure below target is visible here and raised there.

13.7 Feeds Equilibrium. Where the client's hours actually go is one half of the comparison against what the client said matters.

13.8 Fed by Career Development, Schedule. The work schedule supplies planned shifts. This folder holds what was worked.

13.9 Feeds Finance. A purchase amount from an executed commitment is filed in Finance.

13.10 Read by Integrity. Integrity reads the commitments and outcomes on file here. It covers every commitment, at work and outside work, and holds no second copy.
