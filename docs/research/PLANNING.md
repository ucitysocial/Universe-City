# Research Notes — Human Planning and Goal Follow-Through

**Status:** RESEARCH

This file separates research findings from Universe City product synthesis.

The research does **not** prove one perfect planning interface. It does support several design principles that can inform the Time System.

---

## 1. Intention does not reliably become action

People can strongly intend to do something and still fail to perform it.

A well-studied intervention is the **implementation intention**: specifying when, where, and how action will occur, often in an if-then structure.

Example:

> If it is Tuesday after work, then I will go directly to the gym.

Product implication:

Universe City should help turn vague intentions into realistic specific actions rather than treating "I want to..." as a Plan.

Reference:

- Gollwitzer & Sheeran, implementation intentions and goal achievement: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17295981/

---

## 2. Action planning and coping planning are distinct

**Action planning** specifies how intended behavior will happen.

**Coping planning** anticipates barriers and specifies what to do when they occur.

Product implication:

A strong Plan may contain both:

> Gym at 6:30 after work.

and:

> If work runs more than 30 minutes late, move the workout rather than abandoning the week.

This supports the idea that a successful day may include a successfully executed fallback.

Reference:

- Planning intervention review: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30973747/

---

## 3. Humans underestimate future task time

Planning-fallacy research shows that people often build predictions around optimistic future scenarios and underuse relevant past experience.

Breaking a task into components can improve estimation in some contexts.

Product implication:

Universe City should learn from actual past behavior rather than repeatedly accepting an idealized estimate as truth.

Instead of only:

> Work starts at 9.

learn the chain:

> wake → prepare → leave → commute → arrive.

Reference:

- Buehler, Griffin & Ross / planning fallacy work: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11014050/

---

## 4. Monitoring progress improves goal attainment

A large meta-analysis found that interventions designed to increase progress monitoring improved goal attainment, with stronger effects when progress was physically recorded or reported.

Product implication:

> Did today go as planned?

is not merely an engagement prompt. A lightweight recorded review can be part of effective self-regulation.

Universe City can improve on binary completion tracking by preserving:

**Plan → Actual → Difference → Reason → Adjustment**

Reference:

- Harkin et al., progress monitoring meta-analysis: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/

---

## 5. Goals work better when they belong to the person

Research on self-concordant goals and self-determination suggests that goals aligned with a person's own values/interests and supported by perceived competence/autonomous motivation are more likely to sustain meaningful progress.

Product implication:

Universe City should not substitute its own idea of "productive" for the resident's desired life.

A protected recovery evening may be as valid a Plan target as a workout or job application.

References:

- Self-concordance and goal progress: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12088128/
- Autonomous motivation / perceived competence in behavior change: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32437175/

---

## 6. Desired future + real obstacle can improve planning

Mental contrasting asks people to connect a desired future with the real obstacle that currently prevents it. Combined approaches such as mental contrasting with implementation intentions have shown small-to-moderate benefits for goal attainment across studies.

Product implication:

Once a resident says what they want to happen, Universe City can ask:

> What usually gets in the way?

That is more useful than simply adding the goal to a calendar.

Reference:

- Mental contrasting with implementation intentions meta-analysis: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34054628/

---

## 7. Good self-regulation also includes adjustment

Goal pursuit is not always about greater persistence. Goal adjustment/disengagement can be adaptive when a goal or strategy is not working.

Product implication:

Universe City should not encode:

> changed Plan = failure.

A changed Plan is information. Repeated mismatch can justify changing the Plan, Schedule, strategy, or goal.

Reference:

- Recent review/meta-analytic work on goal adjustment: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41233533/

---

# Universe City synthesis

The following is a **product synthesis**, not a published scientific framework.

A useful planning loop may be:

## 1. Start with reality

What already has the resident's time?

- work
- sleep
- commute
- appointments
- care obligations
- recurring maintenance

Do not plan over a life that is already full.

## 2. Ask what the resident wants

What would they like to be different?

Do not assume optimization.

## 3. Choose a small meaningful move

Avoid translating broad goals into extreme frequency.

Example:

"I want to get in shape" does not automatically mean seven gym sessions.

## 4. Place it in real context

When and where can it actually happen given the resident's life?

## 5. Identify likely friction

What usually prevents this?

## 6. Define a fallback when useful

What happens if the expected barrier occurs?

## 7. Review the specific Plan

Did the planned touchpoints happen?

## 8. Learn from the difference

Was this a one-off change or evidence that the system needs updating?

---

# Product principles suggested by the research

- Planning should become **more personalized and less burdensome over time**.
- Do not keep asking about stable behavior forever.
- Use prior actual behavior when making suggestions.
- Ask specific follow-ups from the Plan rather than broad recall questions.
- Treat misses as diagnostic information.
- Distinguish aspiration from commitment.
- Protect resident autonomy.
- Do not reward raw productivity volume.
- Do not make unsupported neuroscience claims about reward/dopamine.
