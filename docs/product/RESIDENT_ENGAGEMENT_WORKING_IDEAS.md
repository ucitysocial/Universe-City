# Resident Engagement and Software Experience — Working Ideas

**Status:** WORKING IDEAS / NOT APPROVED FOR IMPLEMENTATION  
**Origin:** "Universe City Resident Engagement and Software Experience," working document dated October 11, 2026 (17 pages).  
**Scope:** Cross-department resident engagement across all 48 folders. Time is the existing daily planning and coordination foundation; Genesis is outside this document's central scope.  
**Authority:** This document does **not** supersede `LANGUAGE_STANDARD.md`, approved product specifications, folder specifications, or operations policies. Check the current live governing files before implementing anything.  
**Maintenance:** Preserve ideas as proposals, distinguish founder directions from unapproved designs, and log changes with a date. This repository Markdown is a structured working reference drawn from the original Word working document, not a claim that these proposals are approved.

## 1. Product proposition and resident value

Universe City should accept ordinary, free-form discussion about a resident's life. Residents may mention appointments, ideas, questions, reminders, concerns, preferences, or changes without first choosing a Department or folder. Software interprets and prepares relevant work; the **agent is a human person**, able to review, edit, intervene and personally respond. Software must never impersonate the agent.

The proposed reason for residents to return is **practical continuity**, not messages, logins or artificial chores: accurate memory, useful retrieval, maintained information, supported decisions and better management of changing commitments. The service is governed by its four Departments and 48 folders; it is not intended to be unlimited entertainment or general concierge service.

Relevant existing terminology and constraints must come from the current `LANGUAGE_STANDARD.md`. The current working premise is that Time, Inventory, Salary and Standards are the operational folders; future-state examples involving other folders must not be represented as deployed. The File is the maintained whole of information across folders. Resident-facing and internal agent language differ as defined in the Language Standard. No pricing promise belongs in public copy absent governing approval.

## 2. Natural conversation without premature filing

The resident can say:
- "I have an appointment coming up." Ask only for essential missing appointment details; propose scheduling or reminders.
- "Remind me about X." Clarify timing only if necessary; keep the follow-up appropriate.
- "I need to make time for something." Check the existing Time Schedule and offer genuinely feasible windows.
- "My manager irritated me; I need to vent." Acknowledge and listen; do **not** automatically file a complaint or conclude the resident requests career action.
- "I saw something interesting; I'd like to research it." Preserve the interest in an authorized way, without creating an invented deadline.
- "I want today to go as planned." Show planned work and reported outcomes without making up completion or punishing changed plans.

Distinguish **a passing thought, question, preference, emotion, possibility, decision, commitment and observed event**. Do not convert every message into an obligation. Information retention rules, confirmation requirements and the visibility of inferences remain design questions. A resident statement, an inferred interpretation, and an estimate must remain distinguishable.

## 3. Cross-department practical continuity

Useful reasons to engage include a known obligation approaching, a meaningful change, a choice, a comparison with earlier information, or continuity of the human agent relationship.

- **Agency Assessment:** changed commitments, appointments, deadlines, relevant information updates.
- **Housing Stability:** possessions, repairs, household situations, transportation, moves.
- **Career Development:** shifts, pay changes, workplace issues, offers, professional commitments.
- **Life Management:** resident-selected standards, relationships, priorities and boundaries.

All are examples of the future full experience; do not claim unbuilt folder functionality exists.

## 4. Contact cadence and genuine engagement

Possible useful moments:
- Daily, **when useful**: today's Plan/Schedule and relevant changes.
- Weekly: coming seven-day commitments and choices.
- Monthly: work actually performed, File maintenance and Grade under governing rules.
- Event-driven: a consequential change, approaching obligation or unresolved decision.
- Resident-initiated: questions, venting, notes, plans, corrections.

Quiet days are valid. Do not demand artificial daily attendance, fabricate work for engagement or equate message count or streaks with progress. A low Start or Position must not itself impose more notifications. Contact respects resident preferences, established commitments, meaningful consequences and timing.

## 5. Authentic progress, without performance theater

Explore a calm visual experience of the day unfolding: scheduled commitments, changed plans, confirmed outcomes, and genuinely open matters. One possible display is "2 of 5 commitments confirmed," but never treat an unchecked box as evidence of failure or a checked box as an observed outcome. Preserve the original Plan when revised; separate intended, completed, partially executed, changed, missed and unknown using **actual governing Time statuses**, not a new status taxonomy invented in this document.

Keep provenance (reported, confirmed, proposed, unknown). Do not reward the volume of submissions or imply that a quiet resident has done nothing. Test practical value rather than only application time.

## 6. Input-to-action proposal

An illustrative software-assisted sequence:
1. Receive the natural-language message.
2. Interpret its type and uncertainty.
3. Retrieve relevant, dated information already on File.
4. Prepare a response, question, possible File change or follow-up.
5. Apply approval rules: authorized automatic action, resident confirmation, or human agent review.
6. Execute only permitted actions, distinguishing proposal from completion and recording what actually happened.
7. Follow up on consequential or date-relevant open items, not at an arbitrary rhythm.

Approval mechanics must defer to existing Language Standard, architecture and Operations. There is an **unresolved decision** about keeping spontaneous language as conversation history, structured File facts after confirmation, or both.

## 7. Human agent console

The proposed console should let the human agent see clients who need attention, without reading every routine message. Possible functions: relevant conversation/context; suggested folder routing; clearly pending drafted responses; approve/edit/send personally/hold/reject/request more information; proposed File change diffs; questions, deadlines and permissions; audit trail differentiating software preparation, client permission, agent action and execution.

These are design proposals, not deployed features.

## 8. Illustrative Genesis testing, not primary product scope

Genesis may simulate interactions to test the real resident model; it is not a prerequisite for advancing the resident product. In one fictional example, a resident reports reduced work hours and anxiety about upcoming rent. Software might prepare relevant Time/Employment/Finance/Salary context and one necessary scheduling question, while avoiding invented pay rates, dates or automatic conclusions.

A proposed test uses **12 fictional residents over 30 days**, with independent daily life events, quiet days, changed intentions, conflicts, missed work, household events and unsolicited venting. Observe whether follow-ups were appropriate, proposed actions correct, assumptions grounded, and resident/agent work reduced. Test false assumptions, privacy problems, over-contact, schedule changes without authorization and software impersonation. These numbers are **test ideas**, not operational requirements.

## 9. Sustainable operation

A prior internal discussion mentioned a tentative $12/week target; this is not an approved public price. Sustainable human-review time, escalation, retention, permission scopes, payment operations and support boundaries remain open.

Potential supporting research topics: personal information management, prospective memory, reminders, just-in-time interventions, self-monitoring, notification fatigue, user control over memory, relationship continuity and human-in-the-loop approval.

## 10. Portal layout ideas

The founder preferred **flexible focused navigation ("Concept C")** over a visually overwhelming card-heavy overview ("Concept B").

Working direction:
- **Today:** initially four Department squares, potentially with a meaningful attention indication.
- **Department:** its twelve folders, with appropriate per-folder statuses.
- **Folder:** its own record and working System (e.g., Time's seven-day Schedule; Inventory; Salary; Standards).
- **Conversation:** readily accessible, persistent place for discussion regardless of folder.
- **My File:** access to the maintained information across folders.
- **About Me:** a **conversation mode**, not a fifth Department or extra database.

Red/amber/green attention statuses are **unapproved visual hypotheses**, not assessments of a resident's worth, Position, or whether all folders are established. Red might mean timely attention; amber, follow-up; green, no known alert. Exact thresholds, expiry, inheritance and accessibility need design and testing. Always pair color with understandable text. Open questions include last-view persistence, whether Today is default, embedded conversation access and presentation of history.

## 11. About Me, information, and memory

Residents can converse voluntarily about favorites, dislikes, interests, beliefs, relationships, history, aspirations and appropriate adult subjects. This is not another twelve-question assessment. Learning also happens naturally during ordinary conversations.

The File remains the maintained information home under the governing rules. Residents should be able to inspect and correct material information. Casual remarks and agent interpretations must not silently become permanent asserted facts. Intimate topics require context and consent; minor-facing/educational contexts require distinct safeguards.

## 12. Pattern identification as an agent responsibility

A central founder direction: **the agent should notice meaningful patterns**, supported by dated and attributable observations, across commitments, decisions, habits, preferences, communication and changed circumstances. Software can prepare evidence-based hypotheses for the agent. An unusual single conversation or moment of frustration must **not** create a lasting personality or clinical conclusion.

Example: if four of six Friday evening commitments were rescheduled, the agent could observe that verified pattern and ask whether Friday evenings remain practical. Never invent counts or causes. Re-evaluate patterns as circumstances change. Consequential interpretations require an appropriate transparency/correction process; exact privacy, retention and disclosure requirements remain proposals.

## 13. How the agent selects a question

The Language Standard's revised §3.20 says Position **does not** dictate work order. The next action considers missing information, commitments, importance, consequences and preferences. Understand the resident's actual request, retrieve relevant File details, identify what is unknown, and ask **one useful question at a time**. If a permitted action already can be taken, there may be **zero** questions.

A new Friday date could invite a simple timing question, a clarification about an existing Friday commitment, or a budget question depending on what is actually on File. The most consequential item is not necessarily the correct *first* question; relevance, timing and conversational fit matter. Proactive pattern interruptions require judgment.

## 14. Conversational pacing — founder direction, October 11, 2026

**Acknowledge what the resident said before shifting into analysis.** A simple, neutral response is sufficient; no performative excitement is needed.

For a **nonurgent** observed pattern, let the conversation develop naturally. Roughly **one to three responses** may precede a useful pattern observation, depending on context; this is **not a quota**. Then connect the observation to a verified commitment/consequence and help the resident plan around the two.

**Message size:** Normally ordinary text-message length, **one meaningful thought per message**. Do not combine acknowledgement, pattern and action into a lecture. Do not send empty additional messages merely to drive engagement.

Illustrative fictional exchange:
> Resident: "I want to go on a date Friday night."  
> Agent: "Okay, what are you thinking for Friday?"  
> Resident: "Dinner and drinks. I might stay out late."  
> Agent (message 1): "Got it."  
> Agent (message 2): "One thing worth considering. You missed three Saturday morning commitments after staying out late on Fridays."  
> Agent (message 3): "You have work Saturday at 8:00 AM. We need to account for that when planning Friday."

The example **requires those past missed commitments and Saturday shift to be genuinely supported by the fictional File**. Confirmed obligations/consequences justify clearer language; uncertainty calls for a question. Resident retains decision authority. Urgent deadlines or major confirmed consequences should be raised promptly, without waiting for conversational small talk.

Before sending software-prepared agent communication, ask: Was the present subject acknowledged? Is this the right moment for the pattern? Is its evidence genuine and dated? Is the message concise? Are the consequences truly known? Does it help with the new activity *and* established commitments? Is it within authorized folder scope? Would one question be better?

## 15. October 11 synthesis — expand what Universe City already defines

**Founder instruction:** Engagement research must expand the actual Universe City model rather than proposing an alternative intake, new daily planning system, or universal routine. The next design work is how the established systems feel while residents use them, especially during a real day.

### 15.1 Authoritative existing foundation

The following is **already decided at the specification/product-direction level**, not newly proposed here.

- `docs/PRODUCT_BIBLE.md` and `docs/DECISIONS.md`: Universe City builds and maintains the resident's systems. The resident provides circumstances, decisions, corrections, and actions only they can carry out. One File spans 48 folders; Time, Inventory, Salary, and Standards are foundation folders.
- `docs/systems/TIME.md` Version 4: Time handles **planning, execution, review, and follow through**. The agent builds the seven day schedule from conversation; clients can also edit it by hand. No one has to fill an empty calendar. The schedule represents the recurring week; the **Plan** holds commitments for a particular day.
- Time starts with actual circumstances and collects only what is needed. Every commitment has a scheduled block and conditions as relevant: Timing, Completion, Consequences, Standards, and Follow through. These are **internal conditions, not a fixed questionnaire**.
- Time separates block from commitment, commitment execution from ongoing work, planned from done, and estimated from observed. A changed time alone does not prove that execution failed. Unreported completion remains unknown.
- Time provides optional **Morning Plan**, selective contact during the day, and regular **Daily Close**. The client controls amount and timing of contact. Daily Close reviews what actually happened and confirms the next day's Plan.
- A commitment arising in another folder is linked into Time when it needs scheduling. The original folder continues to own the subject information. Time coordinates it; do not duplicate records.
- Standing permissions cover specified routine work. Software can carry out that work and must identify automated messages honestly. Interpretive changes and decisions beyond permission require the applicable approval process.
- `docs/product/RESIDENT_INTERFACE.md` and `docs/DECISIONS.md`: the Resident Portal has an organized File, useful home, and persistent agent communication. Current desktop shell direction is File at left, main content in the center, agent at right. The four-Department-square Today view remains an **engagement concept to reconcile**, not a replacement for that locked shell.
- `docs/product/AGENT_MODEL.md`: follow-up is selective. The service should get quieter as stable behaviors are understood. Avoid creating a second inbox or routine questions with no purpose.
- `docs/research/PLANNING.md`: distinguishes intentions and action, planning for friction, actual-versus-planned review, and learning from adjustment.

**Implementation reality is separate.** `docs/systems/TIME_OPERATIONS.md` identifies several defined but unbuilt mechanics for commitments, follow ups, and ongoing work. `app/(resident)/portal/time/page.tsx`, `components/FirstPlan.tsx`, and `lib/domain/time.ts` retain earlier implementation concepts. Do not infer a feature is live simply because it is specified, or treat earlier UI code as superseding Time Version 4.

### 15.2 Founder concept: schedule first, with progressive detail

The founder prefers opening into the **schedule or daily Plan first**, rather than an automatic agent-led conversation, when the resident chooses to view their day.

The schedule provides the broad periods of time. Selecting a block gradually reveals:

1. **Day overview:** time, label, and the main commitments; calm enough to scan at once.
2. **Expanded block:** what the resident plans to execute in that period; relevant steps and linked ongoing work.
3. **Connected information:** only the relevant detail from other folders, with clear distinction between an established commitment, an unresolved follow up, and a suggestion.
4. **Source folder:** the resident can inspect the actual record or system when more detail is needed.

Examples are **illustrative only**, never default assumptions about what every resident does:

- A self-described getting-ready block might contain washing up, clothes, breakfast, and packing. These need not each occupy separate large time blocks.
- A paid work block might make the relevant Career Development information available: a Key Performance Indicators review, a Resources question for HR, an Information Technology problem, or an Advancement discussion. Such items only appear when supported by the resident's File or active work; not all folder systems are operational.
- A person attending school, caring for another person, unemployed, retired, between jobs, or with irregular hours may have completely different blocks. **No universal morning, evening, night, employment, or school routine.**

The point is **progressive disclosure**, not new categories of required life activity. Large blocks provide orientation; relevant information appears when the resident asks to go deeper. Folder links should reuse actual records and permissions rather than duplicate the information inside the schedule.

### 15.3 Optional activity checklists and execution experience

A resident may want to mark the individual steps of an established commitment as they work through it. The experience could be satisfying because it helps them see which work remains and where they are in their Plan.

**Still a concept to research:** some blocks may have several actionable steps while others may contain one action, passive time, rest, or no checklist at all. A step shown under an expanded block should be a real reported/planned action, a clearly marked suggested action, or linked ongoing work. Do not generate obligatory micro-tasks for engagement. Do not imply an unchecked item is missed, or that ticking a box proves all completion conditions were met without the governing evidence/confirmation rule.

Time Version 4 already supports the distinction between a commitment being executed and other follow through remaining open. **Proposed presentation:** a compact progress indicator may summarize which *confirmed or resident-reported* steps are done, without grading the resident by quantity.

### 15.4 Optional focus session or progress timer

**Founder idea, exploratory:** Residents who find execution support motivating could start an activity session from an expanded time block, such as `Start morning` or `Start work`. The interface could show calm elapsed progress, a current step, and the remaining planned actions. The design must avoid a daunting countdown, urgency theatrics, or a race against the clock.

**It is optional.** Many clients neither need nor want a timer. They must retain the full schedule, checklist (when applicable), manual editing, and conversational reporting **without** running a session.

Potential modes to investigate, rather than prescribe:
- no timer at all; ordinary schedule navigation and updates;
- a subtle elapsed-time indicator;
- progress through acknowledged steps;
- optional comparison of planned versus reported actual duration to help correct future schedules.

A session starting or reaching its allotted duration does **not** mean the commitment was executed. The client or authorized evidence supplies the outcome. Estimates remain estimates until a relevant observed report is available. Pause, interruptions, accessibility, and use on shared or low-power devices need research.

**Device independence:** The primary interaction must work in the browser on desktop, tablet, or phone without requiring an installed app. A resident might have no smartphone. A mobile-only distraction restriction cannot be a condition of participating in Time.

**Optional future phone feature:** The founder also imagined reducing phone distractions while a focus session is active. Explore only as an explicit, reversible, device-supported opt-in. A website cannot universally lock a person's phone or block all other apps. Platform permissions and accessibility differ. Do not design a coercive lock, prevent calls or access to essential functions, or claim this exists.

### 15.5 How expansion supports the agent's existing job

The visual block and the agent are coordinated views of the same File. The agent can already apply Time Version 4's rules to check:
- What the client actually planned and authorized.
- What they have reported executing, partly executing, changing, or not executing.
- Which consequences, minimums, linked folder requirements, and follow ups matter.
- Whether a competing commitment requires the client's decision.
- Whether a relevant pattern deserves a short, appropriately timed observation.

The agent is not obliged to talk merely because the resident expands a block, ticks a step, or begins a focus session. Clear progress can be shown silently. Software can prepare permitted changes; only approved or authorized actions actually change the File.

### 15.6 Priority engagement questions to investigate

1. What precisely appears at each expansion level, without duplicating canonical File content?
2. Should the expanded block show only **scheduled commitments**, or also clearly distinguished **linked open follow ups and optional suggestions**? How does the resident prevent clutter?
3. Which blocks benefit from component steps or checklists, and how are those steps established naturally rather than imposed?
4. When does checking a step constitute a resident report of execution, and when is it merely a visual marker? How does this reconcile with Time's Completion condition and four independent outcome fields?
5. What display of elapsed time feels supportive versus distracting, and how can a client turn it off permanently?
6. How does a session move between phone and web or survive loss of connection without corrupting observed time?
7. How should the agent use repeated **reported** durations to propose a schedule correction without treating inferred time as confirmed observation?
8. How does the experience change for someone with no regular routine or fixed daily schedule, while preserving the same Time rules?
9. Does the optional timer measurably improve follow through for interested residents compared with checklist-only or schedule-only experiences?
10. How do time blocks link into future Career Development and other folders without implying all 48 are presently implemented?


## 16. Guided Mode — detailed exploratory concept, October 11, 2026

**Origin:** Founder and younger brother's JARVIS-like concept, developed through discussion. **Status: WORKING IDEA / RESEARCH.** Guided Mode is an *optional way to execute work already established in Time and relevant folders*. It does not redefine Time, replace Daily Close or Morning Plan, require residents to have routines, or imply an alarm/voice/blocking implementation exists.

### 16.1 Experience and participation choices

The resident can use their Plan in several ways, without penalty or hierarchy of worth:

- **Schedule only:** Look at the day, expand blocks when useful, manually update the schedule, or converse with the agent. No narration, session, checklist, or tracking requirement.
- **Screen-guided:** Start any selected block; show the present step, next step, and meaningful progress. Use touch, keyboard, or other accessible controls.
- **Voice-guided:** Optionally put on headphones and hear spoken briefings, guidance, curriculum, and useful time reminders. Voice may initiate prompts automatically, rather than waiting for a wake phrase each time. The client can interrupt, answer by voice when practical, or use visible controls.
- **Distraction support:** An additional, separate opt-in possibility that may restrict selected distractions on supported devices. It is not a prerequisite of participation or a condition for proving completion.

The same framework could support getting ready, work, school, commuting, household tasks, caring responsibilities, study, errands, exercise, preparing for an appointment, or any other resident-established schedule block. Do not assume a fixed morning, evening, paid employment, or conventional household routine. Some blocks may have no steps and require no guidance.

The proposed name **Guided Mode** is a working interface label only. The approved Time terms, including block, commitment, Plan, follow up, execution, and ongoing work, remain unchanged.

### 16.2 Opening: a brief daily Plan refresher, then begin

The founder prefers **schedule first**. When a resident deliberately starts a guided day or morning, a useful spoken overview can create direction without interrogating them upon waking or reading a long list of every item.

The briefing might cover, in priority order:

1. The most important confirmed commitments and timing already in the day's Plan.
2. The next firm appointment or departure and the actual time available until it.
3. Preparation, travel, or recovery blocks that are recorded as costs of those commitments.
4. One or two important decisions or relevant follow ups that may affect execution.
5. A direct, easy transition into the first activity.

**Example with fictional on-file details:** “Good morning. You have work at nine, groceries after work, and dinner with your sister tonight. We need to leave by eight thirty. You've got an hour and a half before departure. Let's start with getting ready.”

This should sound like an agent who understands the whole day, not an exhaustive status report. The point of hearing a busy Plan is to recognize what needs doing and begin. The resident may choose whether the briefing plays at all and how much detail it gives. No routine question is required unless an answer will change the Plan or determine an authorized action.

### 16.3 Activity-aware narration: listening versus responding

Founder direction: **Do not ask for an answer when someone's mouth or hands are occupied.** During a shower, brushing teeth, driving, carrying items, or similar activities, spoken reminders and education may still be appropriate but an interactive question may not be.

Proposed distinction:

- **Briefing:** Announce the actual Plan, time available, important preparation and imminent commitments.
- **Guidance:** Narrate current steps, explain why a step matters, and provide calm progress updates. The listener may remain silent.
- **Conversation:** Ask for decisions, clarification, confirmation, or personal preferences at moments the resident can comfortably respond.

The agent may deliberately hold a nonurgent HR question, date-planning detail, or personal follow up until breakfast, a transition, or another available moment. Avoid assuming the interface can detect a shower, busy hands, or attention; use the *known scheduled activity, resident preferences and optional resident controls*, and keep any inferred state uncertain. When driving, narration must avoid distracting interactions and prioritize safety.

Speech should not be constant simply because speech is available. Adjustable quiet periods, a pause, skip, mute, repeat, and a way to switch to visual-only operation matter. Residents can opt out of automated prompts.

### 16.4 Time awareness without a daunting countdown

The founder wants to know **how much usable time remains before the next commitment**, including time for travel, changing clothes, other preparation, and any inflexible departure. That helps them prioritize rather than discover too late that they have run out of time.

Example fictional arrangement: it is 7:00 AM; work is at 9:00 AM; the recorded commute requires leaving at 8:30 AM. There are **90 minutes until departure**, not 120 minutes of preparation time. The established Plan might contain thirty minutes for showering and dressing, twenty for breakfast, ten for packing, and thirty of available flexibility. The agent can express those facts simply, with a calm visual summary.

During the session, useful reminders can occur **throughout**, based on an approaching deadline, departure, the amount of planned work remaining and the resident's contact preferences. Possible voice messages:

> “You've got an hour before you need to leave.”

> “Thirty minutes until departure. Getting dressed and packing are still on your Plan.”

These must be derived from actual, timezone-aware timings and known or explicitly estimated durations. Do not invent commute length or pretend that a variable commute is fixed. Show whether a figure is planned, estimated, or observed. Avoid an intimidating large red countdown, constant pressure, or repeated prompts without new information.

**Optional display choices:** a gentle “time until next commitment” readout, elapsed-time indicator, progress through confirmed steps, or no timer. The person may prefer spoken time cues without staring at numbers.

### 16.5 Progression can be proactive or resident-controlled

The founder wants a **combination**:

- The experience can tell the resident what comes next and offer an estimated time for the step.
- When done, the resident can say “I'm finished” or activate a button to move on immediately.
- The interface can check in gently if the time exceeds the resident's planned or previously reported duration.
- If extra time is needed, the resident can say so or select an extension without restarting the day or triggering a failure state.
- A narration sequence can proceed automatically when no reply is needed. Do not mark the *commitment* executed just because narration finished or a timer reached zero.

There is no requirement to repeatedly ask “Are you done?” Each question must have an actual purpose in Time's rules. Source evidence, reporting, and the Completion condition still govern execution. A checklist tick may be a resident report of a step; whether it establishes full commitment execution depends on its stated conditions and must be researched.

### 16.6 Curriculum-guided activity: teaching while doing

Founder example: a 15-minute guided shower with spoken instructions and brief explanations of *why* actions matter, or a guided toothbrushing session with practical technique. This could make activities more interesting and make them easier to begin, especially when motivation is low. Other possible future contexts include household cleaning, education/study, preparation for work, and Career Development actions.

**This must use the actual appropriate folder's approved curriculum and research as its factual foundation**, and should not invent health or hygiene claims. Time establishes *when* an action happens and how it relates to commitments. The relevant folder owns its *subject* and educational content. If the relevant curriculum is unapproved or the folder is not operational, treat the lesson as future exploration and do not represent it as an official live lesson.

Proposed user-selectable **depths of narration**:

- **Basic guidance:** concise starting cue, transitions, and timing when relevant.
- **Learn as you go:** step-level narration plus short explanations selected from approved material.
- **Full guided lesson:** longer educational narration and carefully timed demonstrations/check-ins, only where the resident specifically wants more detail.

The resident can switch levels by activity or by session. A full explanation is not required to obtain the benefits of the Time schedule. Accessibility and suitability matter: someone may prefer short text prompts, silence, translated material when available, or no lesson.

### 16.7 Progressive, varied scripts rather than repetitive playback

**Explicit founder preference: progression.** The resident should **not hear the exact same script every time**. Investigate **approved script frameworks** from which context-specific versions can be prepared, rather than asking a model to invent a new lesson without a factual source.

A proposed script framework can contain:

1. **Purpose and required facts:** what the session actually teaches or supports; the approved folder source.
2. **Core verified steps:** procedural instructions or essential safety/accuracy information which must not be changed by improvisation.
3. **Optional educational material:** rotating concepts, deeper explanations, small contextual examples, and reminders of prior lessons.
4. **Relevant File context:** current Plan, upcoming appointments or commitments, the resident's stated preferences and standards, linked open follow ups, and previous session feedback.
5. **Narration timing:** when to brief, give noninteractive instruction, ask a real question, or stay silent.
6. **Progression rules:** initial detailed walkthroughs, later lighter touch, new educational material or refreshers when useful, more detail on request, and adaptation when the resident reports difficulty.
7. **Reviewability:** identifiable source and version so the human agent can inspect the basis of an educational message and correct erroneous context.

Progression should follow **evidence of the resident's familiarity or stated preference**, not an automatic assumption that completing three sessions means mastery. Some activities benefit from consistent steps but varied explanation. The resident can always request a repeat of a previous explanation.

### 16.8 Relevant whole-life context can be woven into a session

The founder strongly endorses references to relevant information from **other folders** and the File during Guided Mode. Examples include an upcoming date, an HR benefits question, an appointment, or preparation for a professional commitment. This should make guidance feel attentive to the resident's life rather than like a generic prerecorded video.

**Founder example, not a default beauty standard:** while showering the day before a date, the guidance might mention extra grooming **only if the resident's File supports it as a preference or stated plan**, e.g. “You've got your date tomorrow. You mentioned wanting extra grooming beforehand; want to make time for that?” The agent should ask when the resident can respond, not while their mouth is occupied or showering. If the resident says they prefer to do it tomorrow, the Plan can be adjusted under the existing permissions. Do not assume shaving is necessary for a date.

Unrelated-but-relevant information should be *timed*, not dumped into the session. A 7:00 AM briefing may mention the one or two most useful points; other items can be introduced later at a better activity transition. No requirement to fill silence with conversation.

**Proactive pattern awareness** still applies: an evidence-based pattern involving repeatedly missed next-day commitments may deserve mention while planning an evening out, but with natural acknowledgement and conversational pacing where circumstances are nonurgent.

### 16.9 The real value: revise the Plan when execution goes sideways

**Explicit founder direction:** when the client feels rushed or overwhelmed, the agent helps them **think through what to do next**. This is central to representation, not a mere timer feature.

Example, fiction: the resident planned a thirty-minute shower, took forty-five minutes, and now has 25 minutes until departure. They still need to dress, pack and eat. Instead of a buzzer or scolding, the agent can say, in separate brief messages:

> “You've got 25 minutes until you need to leave.”

> “Getting dressed and packing your bag are still on your Plan.”

> “We can move breakfast until later. Let's get you dressed first.”

This example only works if moving breakfast is genuinely compatible with the resident's stated needs, other commitments, and standards. A real agent must not assume that eating is optional for a particular client, or that a dependent activity can be skipped.

The process to investigate:

1. Identify what has actually happened and what is still **unknown**. Elapsed time alone does not prove a task remains incomplete.
2. Recalculate the remaining time before the next fixed commitment, including preparation/travel costs already on file.
3. Check the recorded importance, consequences, timing flexibility, minimums, and linked unfinished work.
4. **Prepare one workable next action** or a small number of meaningful alternatives rather than requiring the resident to perform all the planning while stressed.
5. Carry out changes already covered by standing permission; seek the client's decision for competing commitments, newly adopted obligations, or other changes reserved for them under Time §5.6.
6. Keep completed work executed; reschedule follow ups or ongoing work still open; report what was changed and preserve provenance.
7. Revisit the Plan during Daily Close and propose improved future time estimates based on actual reports.

**Distinguish two conditions:** *behind schedule* needs a practical revision of timing and work; *overwhelmed even with sufficient time* may need one immediately manageable action before the full Plan is presented again.

Example of the latter: resident says, “I have so much to do. I don't know where to start.” The agent checks the already confirmed Plan, relevant consequences and resident preferences, and can offer one grounded action: “Okay. Let's start with getting dressed.” That is a suggested starting action, not a claim that the resident has executed it.

The resident decides what to do; the agent provides context and practical organization. Avoid guilt, compliance games, or turning distress into extra engagement requirements. For residents who do not want narration, comparable Plan-revision help must remain available through screen and ordinary conversation.

### 16.10 Alarms, headphones, web availability and blocking constraints

The younger brother's illustrative “JARVIS” idea includes Universe City itself being the morning alarm, a day briefing, and optional hands-free progression through the schedule.

**This is an exploratory experience, not a present capability.** A normal browser cannot guarantee reliable device wake alarms, locked-screen continuous listening, or system-wide application blocking across every operating system. Native mobile, browser/PWA, permissions, platform-specific notification, audio and accessibility constraints need separate investigation.

A resident may not own a smartphone or may primarily use a computer. Guided Mode should offer a usable screen-based experience without headphones, microphones, permissions, or device restrictions. A voice-led session can begin when the resident connects headphones if desired; auto-detection and playback rules need research. Any blocking is **opt-in, reversible, selective**, and must preserve emergency and essential communication. Residents can bypass restrictions, so the motivation must come from useful guidance, autonomy, visible progress, and a Plan that works, not fear of losing device access.

### 16.11 Questions to research before an implementation proposal

1. How can the existing schedule and Plan represent nested steps without creating a duplicate second schedule or altering Time's defined data types prematurely?
2. What is the smallest usable Guided Mode experience on a web browser? Which capabilities genuinely require native mobile?
3. Which voice experiences are viable during device lock, with wired/Bluetooth headphones, interrupted network access, no microphone, or other accessibility needs?
4. How is the narration paced around no-reply activities; how can someone say “repeat,” “skip,” “done,” “more time,” or “quiet” without interruption?
5. How does time-remaining guidance account for unknown or estimated durations and departure costs, without false precision?
6. How should optional focus/distraction restrictions respect choice, emergency exceptions, and third-party platform limitations?
7. How are curriculum scripts governed, sourced, reviewed, versioned and advanced through new lessons without repeated stale narration or hallucinated instruction?
8. How does the resident opt into or out of bringing cross-folder reminders and sensitive personal context into audio, particularly where others might hear headphones/speakers?
9. When does a step completion require evidence or client confirmation, and what may software update under standing permissions?
10. What meaningful improvements should we test: completed/adjusted commitments, less planning burden, resident understanding of Time, or a resident's ability to resume after being overwhelmed—not raw listening time?
11. How do we distinguish ordinary auto-reminders from contact that must be attributed to the human agent under existing rules?
12. When does the agent intervene proactively, and when should it allow quiet execution?

## 18. Application to the Time Curriculum — advisory only

When designing Time's assessment and lessons:
- Measure planning, execution, review and follow-through under the resident's established conditions.
- Do **not** grade residents down because their agent/Software properly handles administrative work.
- Preserve resident statements separately from agent interpretation, predictions and observed outcomes.
- Use the actual File and commitments to frame lessons; avoid generic moral lessons and needless time tracking.
- Keep contact preferences and actual timing/consequences separate from assessment Position.
- Teach that adapting a Plan need not mean abandoning it, and that relevant pattern observation can help protect existing commitments.
- Preserve ordinary conversational pacing; Curriculum questions do not authorize an interrogation.
- These considerations **inform drafting** but do not override current authoritative Time, Curriculum or Language Standard text.
