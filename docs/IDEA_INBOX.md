## Time / Plan

### Daily Plan as the interaction primitive

- The core Time question may be: **"Did today go as planned?"**
- The resident's Plan creates the follow-up script instead of requiring them to reconstruct their day.
- Example follow-ups:
  - Did you get to work at the time you said?
  - Did you go grocery shopping like you planned?
  - Did you finish the project you wanted to finish at work?
  - Did you make it to the gym?
- A "yes" should be positively acknowledged.
- A "no" is not a failure state; it is exactly the material Universe City needs to learn from.
- The resident can review today and create/confirm tomorrow's Plan in the same evening interaction.
- Possible default cadence: evening close = review today + plan tomorrow.
- Morning may be a lighter "still the plan?" confirmation rather than a second full planning ritual.

### Usual life vs progress

- A good Plan should begin with what normally has the resident's time.
- Then identify whether anything should be intentionally different tomorrow.
- Do not turn a life goal into an extreme daily habit.
- Example: wanting to get in shape does not imply gym seven days a week.
- Need a way to distinguish:
  - what is fixed/normal
  - what maintains life
  - what moves life forward
  - what protects rest/recovery/relationships
- Possible planning model: Fixed / Maintain / Move / Protect. **Not locked.**
- Possible resident question: **"Is there anything you want tomorrow to be different from usual?"**

### Fallback planning

- A real Plan may include what to do if the predictable obstacle appears.
- Example: if work runs more than 30 minutes late, move the workout rather than treating the week as failed.
- A fallback executed successfully may count as successful planning.

### Planning physics

Over time Universe City could learn resident-specific planning patterns such as:

- actual commute usually takes 15 minutes longer than claimed
- after-work errands fail when more than one is stacked
- plans after 8 PM rarely happen
- the gym works when visited before going home
- 30-minute project blocks happen more reliably than two-hour blocks
- Saturday morning household tasks are consistently completed
- certain days reliably require recovery

Suggestions should eventually come from the resident's own evidence rather than generic productivity advice.

### Aspiration vs commitment

- A resident may want five things without actually committing to all five tomorrow.
- Possible distinction: **Want ≠ Plan.**
- A planned item should mean Universe City may reasonably follow up on it.
- Agent could ask: "Which one or two do you actually want tomorrow to be accountable to?"

### Success / reward

- Avoid gamifying productivity volume.
- Success is closer to: **"I said what I wanted the day to look like, then my real day increasingly matched that intention."**
- Protecting rest can be a successful Plan.
- Changing the Plan can be successful.
- Make visible when someone planned something and followed through.
- Avoid unsupported neuroscience claims about dopamine/reward.

## Agent interactions

### Plan-aware outreach

The Agent can see the resident's Plan and use it as a natural reason to communicate.

Possible touchpoints:

**Before**
- "You have a lot planned after work. Still on track for all of it?"
- "You wanted to leave by 5 today. Still the plan?"

**During**
- Only when useful, not as constant surveillance.
- Example during a new active Time challenge: "Were you able to leave when you planned?"

**After**
- "Did you finish that project?"
- "Did you make it to the gym?"
- "Groceries were planned after work. Did that happen?"

The Plan creates meaningful contact without a generic engagement quota.

### Proactive support

- Agent should be able to help protect a Plan before it breaks.
- Example: resident has groceries + gym + laundry stacked after work; Agent may suggest moving one item before the evening collapses.
- Outreach should be based on importance, active system work, uncertainty, and resident preference—not every calendar item.

### Agent as easiest edit surface

Possible interaction:

Resident: "I'm not going to the gym. I promised my sister I'd help her move."

Agent: "Want me to replace the gym block with your sister's move and put gym back on tomorrow?"

The resident should be able to maintain their life conversationally without opening every folder or editing every calendar block manually.

### Follow-up preferences

Residents may differ:

- "Message me before important things."
- "Only follow up on things I keep missing."
- "Don't contact me during work."
- "Check in with me at night."
- "I need accountability on this specific goal."

Need a model for **how the resident wants Universe City to follow up**.

## Resident interface

### Static shell

Strong current direction:

- **Left:** resident File / folder navigation
- **Center:** main dashboard or currently opened folder/session
- **Right:** Agent interaction
- Frame stays static; the center workspace changes.

This is intentionally **not** a floating-window OS.

### Dashboard

There should still be a useful main dashboard.

Possible information:

- today
- schedule/current/next
- money/obligations
- upcoming appointments/deadlines
- needs resident attention
- active system work/tests/challenges
- selected resident-pinned information

Dashboard should be a **view of data already maintained by systems**, not a duplicate source of truth.

### YAPTAIN organizational inspiration

What we want from YAPTAIN is the organization before the studio interface:

- a clear main container
- folder tree
- open folder/file in the main pane
- clickable path/breadcrumb
- bounded sessions
- nested work
- metadata/status/progress
- predictable places where things live

Do **not** copy the broadcast studio, floating windows, outputs, scenes, or taskbar model.

### Visual identity

- Bring back Universe City color.
- Base: black, white, ivory, restrained neutrals.
- Four canonical colors used as organizational accents rather than giant fills:
  - I Agency Assessment — #5C0F1B
  - II Housing Stability — #A07818
  - III Career Development — #4A2A78
  - IV Life Management — #2A4B3C
- YAPTAIN succeeds by threading accent color through a restrained base; Universe City should do the same with meaningful department color.
- Avoid beige/brown/sepia dominance.
- Avoid generic SaaS cards.

## File / sessions

- The permanent File is the organized current record.
- Sessions may explain how the File got to its current state.
- A session does not have to mean an appointment.
- Sessions might begin from:
  - resident work
  - agent-initiated review
  - meaningful resident update
  - completion of a test/challenge
  - scheduled weekly/monthly review
  - human specialist work
- A session may affect more than one folder.
- Potential session sections:
  - Overview
  - Conversation
  - Evidence
  - Questions
  - Decisions
  - Changes
  - Follow-up
- These names are not locked.

## Desk / organizational model from prior work

Ideas still needing deliberate review:

- Every one of the 48 folders can map to a future Desk/specialty.
- AI may "work a desk" through a defined mandate, expertise, limits, context, and handoffs without falsely claiming to be human.
- Future physical Social Clubs may mirror the same four departments / 48 specialties.
- Need Desk charters that could eventually become human job descriptions.
- Potential Desk definition:
  - purpose
  - promise
  - scope
  - responsibilities
  - outcomes
  - knowledge
  - contact/stay-quiet triggers
  - tests/challenges
  - configuration criteria
  - maintenance
  - handoffs
  - expertise
- Jurisdiction matters: the Agent/Desk should not become "Mayor of Everything."
- Human specialists could have caseloads, queues, review rights, escalation rules, and AI-prepared context.

## Resident ownership / effort

Ideas to preserve from prior work:

- Universe City should use the **least-question principle**: do not ask what is already known or can be safely derived.
- Evidence only proves what it directly proves.
  - transaction ≠ reason
  - screen time ≠ waste
  - silence ≠ avoidance
- Daily interaction should get lighter as the system becomes more accurate.
- Stable areas should stop generating repetitive questions.
- Tests and challenges should be used to learn what works.
- Avoid the term "experiments" in resident-facing product language.
- A resident should feel:
  - "I tell you what's going on."
  - "You pay attention."
  - "You keep track."
  - "You help me see things."
  - "You update the system around me."
